# T-ENERGY-2 — which input drifts

Read-and-tabulate; no product, pin or reference moved. Tables sit beside this file.

## 1. The drifting input is the CALORIFIC BASIS. Generation is not in the running.

Every route divides **the same electricity** by a different energy, so the numerator cannot
move one route out of the envelope and leave another in (Table 17 and the Table 21 station
sum agree to ≤0.30%). Energy is mass × calorific value; three candidates remain.

The decisive measurement uses **no calorific value and no heat rate**: NEPRA prints coal used
and net generation side by side, and their ratio is the electricity the fleet got from one
tonne. It falls **2.073 → 1.274 GWh/kt, −38.6%**, as Thar lignite goes 30.6% → 92.0% of coal
mass. The fleet's rank-default NCV falls −39.6%: the two agree, which is what "efficiency
roughly constant" looks like. HDIP's inferred power-coal factor **rises +6.9%** (18.73, 18.73,
25.27, 21.40, 20.02 TJ/Gg); its national factor falls only −15.5%. Neither tracks the rank
shift. That is the drift, and the first two years give it away: Table 3's TOE over HDIP's own
power tonnage is **0.447399 and 0.447424 TOE/t, identical to four significant figures** —
a conversion
constant, not a measured energy.

## 2. Mechanisms tested and rejected (X-rows)

- **A scalar.** The required correction **changes sign** (×1.22 in FY2020-21, ×0.68 in
  FY2023-24); no wrong constant, unit slip or gross/net convention can do that. A gross→net
  step is bounded at ±5% (coal GCV/NCV ≈ 1.05) and makes the panel worse: 4 of 5 outside.
- **Edition vintage.** 4,875,302 TOE is printed identically in the 2021, 2022, 2023 and 2025
  SoIR editions (furnace oil *was* restated; coal never was), and HDIP's power tonnage is
  `unchanged` in every vintage pair across six editions.
- **A wider plant set.** Extra coal raises energy in every year and cannot also lower it, and
  the early years need it raised. The one real hole, Lakhra (150 MW GENCO lignite, GEM
  "operating", absent from Tables 18/20/21), is under 3% of mass and the wrong sign.
- **A rank-weighted HDIP conversion.** The fit returns f_lignite 21.12 > f_imported 20.51
  TJ/Gg — **sign-impossible.**
- **The supply bound.** At the fleet's physical CV, Table 3's FY2023-24 coal energy needs
  **29,528 kt against a national supply of 24,719 kt — 119.5% of all the coal in Pakistan.**

**No single change fixes all five years.** The calorific swap gets closest (1 year out, span
19.9 against 38.6 points); the mass swap leaves 2 out. Only both together — NEPRA's metered
tonnage at the fleet's rank composition — is in band in all five (34.4–38.7, span 4.3). It is
also the only **non-circular** route: the heat-rate route's energy *is* generation × a printed
heat rate, so it restates NEPRA's own efficiencies and cannot test them. The shipped
adjudication does not say so, and privileges that route.

## 3. Power and industry coal CANNOT confirm each other

HDIP's coal consumption **is** its coal supply: the allocation identity closes to ±0 kt in 12
of 14 years, so the consumer split redistributes a fixed total. Over FY2019-20…FY2023-24 the
total moved **−4 kt** while power went **+8,299** and brick+cement **−8,304**. Industrial coal
is a **residual allocation**, not a measurement, and brick is the plug: 8,184/8,678 kt in the
two years NEPRA's stations out-report HDIP's power column, collapsing to 1,129 kt in the year
HDIP out-reports them by 3,320 kt. **Any "offsetting" between power and industry coal is arithmetic, not
evidence** — and it is not a stock change: an apparent-consumption series carries none by
construction, and NEPRA's column is coal *used*.

## 4. Plant level

GWh/kt separates the ranks with no model: lignite 1.08–1.18 in 13 of 13 plant-years, imported
2.13–2.72 in 11 of 13; implied net NCVs (lignite 10.7–13.6, imported 19.1–25.0) sit inside the
IPCC brackets, and Lucky Electric, origin blank in NEPRA, implies 11.1–12.1 where GEM types it
**lignite**. **Sahiwal FY2021-22 reports 1,443.97 kt for 6,881 GWh — 4.77 GWh/kt against its
own 2.44–2.72**, about 1,170 kt missing, which alone drives the plant route's 45.7%. No
K-Electric or GENCO station burns coal in the panel.

## 5. Does the FY2021-22 peak survive?

Indicative; the power-coal lane only is substituted. The **decline survives** everywhere,
−41 to −45 Mt into FY2022-23. The **peak's location does not**: on NEPRA's metered tonnage
FY2020-21 and FY2021-22 become a plateau differing by 0.04–0.19 Mt, and FY2023-24 lands at
474–480, not 494. Of the +14.67 Mt FY2021-22 step in the power-coal lane — itself 80% of the
national rise that year — **+6.99 Mt is HDIP's conversion factor and +7.68 Mt its tonnage**,
while NEPRA's metered tonnage fell 7.3% and net generation 3.5%. Nearly half of the largest
step in the series is allocation.

## 6. Reference selection

The evidence does **not** select one reference, and nothing is moved toward any external
inventory. On the documented criteria — coverage and units, directness of measurement,
internal physical plausibility — C1 is the best-supported *physical* account; Table 3 remains
the only published national control. Both stand as conditional configurations C0–C3; the
choice stays a declared pin re-base.
