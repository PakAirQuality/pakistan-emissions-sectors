\section{Air-Pollutant Co-Emissions}
\label{sec:air}
% ===========================================================================

Open burning of municipal waste is a combustion source in the middle of where people live, so
its air-pollutant emissions matter for reasons its greenhouse-gas emissions do not---though
the burn sites themselves are unmapped, so its geography, like the sector's, is an origin
allocation on population rather than observed release locations. This
section reports them on the same activity basis as Section~\ref{sec:results}: the same
\SI{9.40}{\mega\tonne} of burned waste, through the \citet{andreae2019} garbage-burning
factors, reported as mass and never converted to \COe{}.

\subsection{Where this sits among Pakistani particulate sources}

At \SI{63.85}{\kilo\tonne} of primary \PM{}, open waste burning is the second largest source
in the national inventory that contains it---behind residential solid fuel at
\SI{282}{\kilo\tonne} and ahead of crop-residue burning at \SI{54}{\kilo\tonne} and road
transport at \SI{8.2}{\kilo\tonne} (EMEP/EEA Tier~1 since the air program's factor
refactor).

That ranking is worth stating carefully, because it inverts the greenhouse-gas one. Open
burning is \SI{0.86}{\percent} of national \COe{} and roughly \SI{14}{\percent} of national
primary \PM{}: a category that is nearly negligible for climate and substantial for exposure.
Road transport runs the other way, at \SI{9.03}{\percent} of emissions and a twentieth of the
particulate.

Because every sector's air layer in this inventory is computed from that sector's own
greenhouse-gas activity data, the comparison is a statement about how these sources burn
rather than an artefact of four studies using four different activity bases---which is the
usual reason such comparisons are unreliable in developing-country settings.

\subsection{Black carbon and hydrogen chloride}

Two entries deserve comment because they are specific to waste rather than generic to
combustion.

Black carbon at \SI{9.22}{\kilo\tonne} is a strong short-lived warming agent that this
inventory reports as mass and does not convert to \COe{}, following the convention that its
metrics remain contested. It is reported because omitting it would understate the climate
relevance of open burning even while the greenhouse-gas total for 4C2 is small.

Hydrogen chloride at \SI{18.43}{\kilo\tonne} appears in no other sector of this inventory and
is a direct consequence of burning chlorinated plastics in the open. It is a marker for the
same combustion conditions that produce dioxins and furans, which this inventory does not
estimate and which would need a dedicated study; its presence here is a flag for that gap
rather than a complete account of it.

\subsection{Limitations of this layer}

The burned tonnage carries the full uncertainty of the partition, so every figure above
inherits it. The composition is regional rather than Pakistani, which matters more for
pollutants than for greenhouse gases because plastics and textiles drive the chlorine and much
of the particulate. And the factors describe open burning generically rather than the specific
conditions of Pakistani street-side burning---smouldering, damp, and often mixed with organic
waste---which would raise the incomplete-combustion products relative to these values.
