\section{Supplementary Note S1: Conditional uncertainty and structural cases}
\label{app:uncertainty}

The release samples the waste model's physical inputs and reruns the disposal-history, wastewater and burning calculations for each realisation. Shared waste generation, collection and uncollected fractions are not independently multiplied into category totals. The identities $D+U=G$ and $B+R=U$ are checked before a draw is accepted, where $D$ is disposed waste, $U$ uncollected waste, $G$ generated waste, $B$ burned waste and $R$ unresolved unburned waste. The latter remains an unresolved fate rather than a new emissions pathway.

Positive quantities retain their declared distributions and fractions remain bounded. The effluent N$_2$O factor uses the existing bounded split-triangular treatment in log space over the 2019 Refinement's 0.0005--0.075 range, with 0.005 as its central mode/median. This asymmetric treatment is not replaced by a symmetric percentage around emitted N$_2$O. The 2006 method remains a separate structural case on its own factor range.

The common annual conditional ensemble uses \num{10000} realisations on seed \num{20261014}. For FY2023--24, the reference waste total is \SI{37.45}{\mega\tonne} CO$_2$e and its conditional 2.5th--97.5th percentiles are \SIrange{25.4}{61.5}{\mega\tonne} on AR5. The mean exceeds the deterministic estimate because the sampled pathways are nonlinear and asymmetric. These are parameter percentiles conditional on the admitted sources and selected management interpretation, not bounds on every possible waste inventory.

The air-pollutant ensemble consumes the same burned dry mass realisations before applying pollutant-specific factor uncertainty. It does not sample population, collection or burn fraction a second time. This preserves physical dependence between open-burning GHG and pollutant emissions. Spatial and temporal allocation uncertainty remains outside these national parameter intervals.

Annual changes use paired realisations. Waste management parameters remain persistent across years unless a source explicitly supports an observed change; supported population inputs and the existing disposal state advance through the annual extension. The annual-activity-error dependence case is published separately from the persistent-parameter case. Neither is assigned a probability to combine them into one interval.

The stagnant-sewer versus flowing-discharge interpretation, burning-fraction alternatives and other supported management cases remain structural sensitivities. Their endpoints are not percentile limits and are not selected because an external inventory happens to agree more closely. Unestimated industrial wastewater and other incomplete sources remain coverage gaps rather than uncertainty draws around zero.

The earlier standalone sector ensemble remains a named diagnostic with its own seed, draw count and factor settings. It is not the current common annual conditional product. Approach~1 category screens and correlation-based driver diagnostics answer different questions: neither supplies an additive variance budget for the current nonlinear national ensemble. The most consequential unresolved evidence concerns wastewater fate and effluent release conditions, burned fractions, waste composition and actual treatment or burn locations.
