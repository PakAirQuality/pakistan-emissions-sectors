% ===========================================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
% ===========================================================================

Pakistan's thermal generating fleet is a small, fully licensed, fully metered population:
roughly seventy plant--fuel series across three systems---the CPPA-G market operator's
independent producers, four legacy public generating companies, and the vertically integrated
K-Electric utility in Karachi---burning imported bituminous coal, domestic Thar lignite,
pipeline gas, regasified LNG, furnace oil and diesel. Its regulator publishes both a national
fuel account and, in the same annual report, plant-level fuel and generation tables that
national inventories, including this one until now, have left unread.

This paper reconstructs that fleet plant by plant and forces it to reconcile with the national
statistics above it. The architecture is a control total with a ledger underneath: the
regulator's national fuel table---in tonnes of oil equivalent, already an energy unit---sets
each fuel's magnitude, and a ledger parsed from the plant tables, covering \SI{99.0}{\percent}
of thermal generation, sets the structure beneath it: which plants, burning which coal, in
which province, at what implied efficiency. The reconciliation runs both ways. The ledger
answers questions the national table cannot---the coal blend becomes an observation rather
than an inference---and it interrogates the national table itself, yielding two findings about
Pakistan's official energy statistics that the aggregate figures alone could never expose.

On that basis we report \SI{45.59}{\mega\tonne} \COe{} for FY2023--24 across two IPCC
categories---grid generation (1A1a) at \SI{40.04}{\mega\tonne} and industrial captive gas
self-generation (1A2) at \SI{5.55}{\mega\tonne}---on a single reference year,
\SI{10.2}{\percent} of a ten-sector national inventory, with an air-pollutant layer published
as an explicitly uncontrolled bound. The grid figure is the selected member of a
structural family (\SI{38.45}{} and \SI{44.05}{\mega\tonne} are the
plant-implied-calorific and K-Electric-inclusive readings, the first a fuel-property
alternative and the second a coverage alternative kept apart from it), and no parametric
interval in this paper is quoted without that family beside it.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{A plant fuel ledger} parsed from the regulator's own tables, covering
  \SI{99.0}{\percent} of FY2023--24 thermal generation, validated per fuel against a
  generation table the parser never reads and screened row-by-row against fuel-intensity
  physics (Section~\ref{sec:data}).
\item \textbf{A coal blend read from plant identity} rather than inferred: lignite share
  \num{0.79}, with the one plant whose origin its name does not state carried as bounds
  (\numrange{0.752}{0.835}). On the energy basis this activity is kept in, the blend moves
  the coal factor by \SI{6.8}{\percent} end to end rather than the factor of two it is worth
  per tonne (Sections~\ref{sec:methods} and~\ref{sec:factors}).
\item \textbf{A physical energy closure} between plant meters and the national table. Its
  assumption-free leg---plants metered in MMBTU---implies \SI{48.7}{\percent} net efficiency,
  evidence that the national table is on the net calorific basis the IPCC factors require,
  which had been this sector's largest declared risk (Section~\ref{sec:validation}).
\item \textbf{Two findings about the official statistics}, published rather than absorbed:
  the national coal energy implies a calorific value \num{1.86} times what a
  \SI{92}{\percent}-lignite fleet supports, and the plant sums bracket the national gas row
  only when K-Electric is excluded---each a question worth megatonnes, posed precisely enough
  to be settled by a tariff filing or a scope note (Section~\ref{sec:validation}).
\item \textbf{Captive gas self-generation}, from metered gas reconciling with a 1,105-plant
  register to within \SI{0.01}{} MMCFD, reported as a floor on industrial combustion rather
  than an estimate of it; and \textbf{an air-pollutant layer as a declared uncontrolled
  bound}, with the parameters that would turn it into an estimate named
  (Section~\ref{sec:air}).
\end{enumerate}

Method shared with the other nine sectors---the module contract, the accounting closures,
the correlated-uncertainty treatment---is documented separately \citep{ahmad2026national}.
