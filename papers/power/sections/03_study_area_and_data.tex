\subsection{The fleet, and the ledger that represents it}

Pakistan's thermal fleet is reconstructed here as a plant fuel ledger parsed from the
regulator's own plant tables (State of Industry Report, Tables~18, 21 and~26): 297 rows of
plant $\times$ year $\times$ fuel quantity $\times$ net generation, FY2019--20 to FY2023--24,
across the three systems those tables separate---the public generating companies, the CPPA-G
market's independent producers, and K-Electric. At the FY2023--24 anchor year the ledger
covers \SI{99.0}{\percent} of thermal generation, and it is validated against a table the
parser never reads: summed per fuel, it reproduces the report's own generation-by-fuel table
to within \SI{2.5}{\percent} for every fuel, and coal to \SI{0.2}{} GWh across eight plants.
Every row is additionally screened against fuel-intensity physics, because in an irregular
document a misread column produces impossible plants, not subtle errors.

Units are preserved as the document states them, and they are load-bearing: gas metered in
MMBTU is already energy, so those rows support comparisons with no calorific assumption; gas
metered in MMCFT is a volume needing a heating value nothing here pins; coal and oil are
masses. The distinction decides what each part of the ledger can prove
(Section~\ref{sec:validation}).

\subsection{The captive plant register, and why it matters}

A register of 1,105 physical captive plants was assembled from three sources: the SNGPL
captive fleet (480 plants, Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa gas network), the SSGC geocoded
captive list (557, Sindh and Karachi), and the licensed generation-CPP register (83, national),
deduplicated where a licensed plant is the same physical site as a gas connection.

Its metered gas sums to \SI{288.01}{} MMCFD against OGRA's independently published national
total of \SI{288}{}. Two sources built differently, from utility connection records and from a
regulator's aggregate, agreeing to \SI{0.01}{} MMCFD is the check that they describe the same
fleet---and it is the reason this category is reported at all rather than left inside the gap.

The register also supplies a \emph{measured} provincial split, only the second in this
inventory after fugitive oil and gas: Sindh \SI{166.3}{}, Punjab \SI{93.8}{}, Khyber
Pakhtunkhwa \SI{13.3}{}, Balochistan \SI{1.8}{} MMCFD, with \SI{0.6}{} in the Islamabad
Capital Territory. The four province figures do not exhaust the register: 37 plants
holding \SI{12.3}{} MMCFD carry no province in the connection records, and that residual
is redistributed pro rata over the assigned fleet rather than dropped---dropping it would
apply shares summing to 0.957 and quietly lose four percent of the category
(\texttt{data/province\_shares.csv} declares the treatment). The fleet is dominated by
textile plants (540 of 1,105).

\subsection{Plant-wise generation, for the provincial proxy}

Grid emissions are allocated by plant-wise thermal generation, from a consolidated series
covering grid plants annually 2015--24 with province and district. Only thermal plants enter
the normalisation: hydro, wind, solar and nuclear generate without combustion and must carry
no share of a combustion sector's emissions. \ref{app:provincial} treats what this replaced
and why.

\subsection{Plant-month generation, for the monthly layer}

The national release resolves this sector's annual totals into fiscal months, and the
evidence behind those months is this sector's to document. Two plant-month generation
sources cover the panel, and which year is asked for decides which answers:
FY2015--16 to FY2020--21 ride PBS's Electricity Generation Data (plant-by-plant,
month-by-month), used wherever enough of a subitem's annual control sits in plants
the publication actually labels; FY2021--22 onward ride NEPRA's State-of-Industry
Detail-of-Generation listings, parsed into the same panel
(\texttt{scripts/ingest\_sir\_generation.py}). The SIR lane carries grid coal at
evidence class TE2 (stated plant by plant) and gas at TE3 --- the mixed-labelled
RLNG/HSD combined-cycle group is assigned to gas by the annual control's own
arithmetic, and an inferred fuel is never graded TE2. Coal and gas only,
deliberately: the liquid fuels already ride OCAC's Total Power customer-class row
with directly differenced months, and a generation shape must not displace a sales
record --- the SIR furnace-oil series serves as a published crosscheck against the
OCAC profile instead. Years neither source covers are refused rather than fitted:
FY2016--17 and FY2017--18 gas (PBS labels barely a third of gas generation in those
years) and FY2021--22 gas (the annual-control bound misses the 95-percent rule by
one point) stay flat with the refusal stated. Captive self-generation is
deliberately not on this profile: a plant burning its own gas behind the meter
follows its host industry, not national dispatch. The timing target is
\emph{generation}, and every month ships in the national monthly product with its
class and basis on the row.

\subsection{What this inventory has not obtained}

Fuel quality is the sector's largest data gap. NEPRA's State of Industry Report publishes fuel
quantities but no analysis---no sulphur content, no ash, no calorific value per consignment, and no split of the
combined coal figure between imported and domestic. Nor is there any national record of which
plants carry precipitators or flue-gas desulphurisation, or at what removal efficiency. Those
absences shape Sections~\ref{sec:methods} and \ref{sec:air} respectively.
