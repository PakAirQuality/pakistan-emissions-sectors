We reconstruct Pakistan's thermal generating fleet plant by plant beneath the national fuel
statistics it is normally summarised by, and report electricity-generation emissions of
\SI{45.59}{\mega\tonne} \COe{} for FY2023--24: \SI{40.04}{\mega\tonne} from grid generation
(1A1a; the selected member of a structural family: 38.45 on a fuel-property alternative, 44.05 on a coverage alternative and 57.82 on the legacy published account) and \SI{5.55}{\mega\tonne} from industrial captive gas self-generation (1A2), together
\SI{10.2}{\percent} of a ten-sector national inventory, on a single reference year.

The activity architecture is a control total with a ledger underneath it. The regulator's
national fuel table---in tonnes of oil equivalent, already an energy unit---sets each fuel's
magnitude; a ledger parsed from the same report's plant tables, covering \SI{99.0}{\percent}
of thermal generation and validated per fuel against a generation table the parser never
reads, sets the structure. The ledger turns the fleet's one unpublished composition question,
the imported-bituminous/Thar-lignite blend, from an inference into an observation: mine-mouth
plants are \SI{75.2}{\percent} of coal generation, and the lignite share is \num{0.79} with
its one unresolved plant carried as bounds (\numrange{0.752}{0.835}). On an energy basis that
blend moves the coal factor by \SI{6.8}{\percent} end to end rather than the factor of two it
would be worth per tonne.

Forcing the plants and the national table to reconcile also tests the statistics themselves,
and two findings are published rather than absorbed. Plants metered in MMBTU---already
energy, so no calorific assumption enters---imply \SI{48.7}{\percent} net efficiency,
consistent with the national table being on a net calorific basis, which had been this
sector's largest declared risk. But the national coal energy implies \SI{24.2}{} TJ/Gg over
a fleet that is \SI{92}{\percent} lignite by mass, and the report's own per-plant heat
rates---an LHV channel with Table~3 nowhere in the loop---put the Thar plants at
\SI{11.1}{} TJ/Gg, squarely IPCC lignite: the official conversion is high by a factor of
\num{2.01} on this channel, worth \SI{1.6}{\mega\tonne}; and the plant sums bracket
the national gas row only when the K-Electric system is excluded, while the furnace-oil sums
sit closer to the national row with it included in four of five years, so whether the
official table already contains K-Electric's fuel is not established. The first question is
now decided and the second is not. The sector books
\emph{grid\_stations\_rank\_default} \SI{40.04}{\mega\tonne}, the
stations' own metered coal at default rank calorific values, because that account implies
plausible net fleet efficiencies in nine of nine panel years where the national conversion
manages two; the conversion is retained as
\emph{grid\_nepra\_reference\_legacy} \SI{57.82}{\mega\tonne}, with
\emph{grid\_plant\_cv\_restated} \SI{38.45}{\mega\tonne} and
\emph{grid\_plus\_ke} \SI{44.05}{\mega\tonne} beside them---competing definitions of the
control total, not sensitivities around a settled one, and not of one kind: the restated
case is a fuel-property alternative on the reference's own fleet, and the K-Electric case
is a coverage alternative, priced beside the reference and not added to it, because adding
it would count that fuel twice if the national statistic already contains it. The UNFCCC
filing's \SI{2}{\percent} solid-fuel agreement is NOT counter-evidence on the coal case, as an
earlier edition of this abstract claimed: the filing is compiled from the same energy
balance the national conversion reports, so it agrees with that conversion by provenance.
The parametric interval below is conditional on the selected member.

Captive gas self-generation rests on a register of 1,105 plants, assembled from related administrative records, that sums to
\SI{288.01}{} MMCFD against the regulator's published \SI{288}{}; it is one fuel in one use,
a floor on industrial combustion, not an estimate of it. Air pollutants are published as an
explicitly uncontrolled upper bound---an uncontrolled screening scenario of
\SI{209}{\kilo\tonne} \SOtwo{}, \SI{113}{\kilo\tonne}
\NOx{}, \SI{362}{\kilo\tonne} filterable particulate of which \SI{34}{\kilo\tonne} is
PM\textsubscript{2.5}, each coal rank on its own AP-42 section since the Thar-lignite
split---because no dataset records which controls are fitted where, and
applying an unpublished removal efficiency would invent the number that matters most. The
gas \NOx{} factor blends the boiler and turbine chapters by the ledger's heat-rate
classification of the fleet, \SI{80}{\percent} combined-cycle, with the unresolved share
reported rather than assigned.
