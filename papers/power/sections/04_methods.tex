\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
For station $i$, fuel rank $r$ and gas $g$, coal emissions are
\begin{equation}
E_{g,t}=\sum_{i,r} M_{i,r,t}\,NCV_r\,EF_{g,r}/10^6,
\end{equation}
where $M$ is fuel consumed in tonnes, $NCV$ is TJ per tonne and the factor is kg per TJ; emissions are Gg. Imported bituminous coal uses 25.8 TJ/Gg and lignite 11.9 TJ/Gg. Their carbon factors are 94\,600 and 101\,000 kg \COtwo{}/TJ, respectively. Rank energy is calculated before factors are applied. A generation-weighted rank share does not weight fuel carbon.

Other grid fuels retain their documented national fuel-energy controls and units. Generation and heat rate test fuel plausibility and provide separately labelled alternatives; they do not replace available physical coal consumption. The station-rank reference, fuel-property alternative, legacy published coal conversion and K-Electric coverage alternative are distinct structural cases without assigned probabilities.

Captive gas volume is converted from MMCFD to annual standard volume, then to net fuel energy using the documented gross heating value and gross-to-net ratio. Captive emissions are assigned to IPCC 1.A.2. Main-activity generation is 1.A.1.a, so the same captive fuel is not added again to public power. Combustion factors already incorporate the IPCC oxidation convention; no additional oxidation discount is applied.

K-Electric coverage is tested fuel by fuel against plant and national controls. The reference CPPA/GENCO boundary and the additional-KE case are published separately where the national table's membership remains ambiguous. Annual native \COtwo{}, \CHfour{} and \NtwoO{} masses are retained, and AR5/AR6 aggregates are computed afterwards.
