\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{The managed-soils difference is methodological}

The engine's managed-soils estimate is about half of FAOSTAT's. The difference
primarily reflects the selected direct emission factor. The 2019 Refinement's
dry-climate $\EFone{}=0.005$ was derived largely from \emph{irrigated} dry-climate
fields, making it the best-supported value for the irrigated Indus belt; the
wet-climate value is not supported there, and the empirical crop-weighted estimate
of \citet{cui2021} for Pakistan ($\approx 0.0055$) is close to it. FAOSTAT still
uses the older 2006 aggregate default $\EFone{}=0.010$. At that aggregate value the
engine's managed soils rises to \SI{40.4}{} Mt~\COe{} and the national total to
\SI{235.4}{} (Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity}), closing most of the gap. We therefore
report the disaggregated value as the default and the aggregate value as a
sensitivity, rather than tuning to match the benchmark.

\subsection{Effect of country-specific livestock parameters}

The principal country-specific refinements occur in livestock characterisation
rather than soil factors: the country animal live weights that set nitrogen
excretion (particularly for buffalo) and the FAOSTAT-derived dairy split. This
indicates where survey effort would most improve the inventory --- livestock
characterisation and manure-management shares --- the categories that also carry
the Tier-2 parameters in Table~\ref{tab:tierstatus}.

\subsection{Value of the reconciliation invariant}

Enforcing Eq.~\ref{eq:reconcile} on every run is what makes the maps usable
alongside the reported totals. It also decouples the two design choices that are
often conflated: activity data and emission factors determine the national
magnitude, while the spatial proxies determine only its geographic distribution and
cannot alter the national total. This separation makes
the system auditable --- a reviewer can check the totals and the proxies
independently.

\subsection{What the district resolution adds}

Resolving to districts changes what the inventory can support. The category
decomposition distinguishes two district profiles that a single activity proxy
would blur: the crop-and-livestock canal-belt districts of central Punjab, and a
livestock-dominated outlier such as Karachi, whose ranking comes from the peri-urban
dairy colonies of Malir rather than cropping. This is the same lesson the source-specific-proxy
literature reaches from the opposite direction --- that where different sources
dominate, one generic proxy misplaces emissions --- and it is why the livestock
split is put on real census head-counts rather than a gridded surface. The
independent crop-record check (Section~\ref{sec:validation-district}) then tells us
where the district geography is trustworthy: strong for rice and, via wheat area,
for managed soils in Punjab and Balochistan; weak in Sindh, where the wheat
footprint is the wrong reference; and simply unvalidated for the categories with no
independent district statistic. Reporting that unevenness, rather than a single
headline correlation, is what keeps the district layer honest.

\subsection{Implications for inventories in data-scarce settings}

The approach generalises beyond Pakistan. Combining coarse but reliable
national/provincial control totals with independent, stream-specific spatial
proxies --- and substituting a dedicated enumeration (here, the livestock census)
for the one category where a generic proxy is weakest --- addresses a structural
problem common to most low- and middle-income inventories, where activity data are
published only at a coarse administrative resolution. The reconciliation invariant
keeps the resulting maps mass-conserving and consistent with the reported totals,
so spatial detail cannot alter the reported national figure. Where the method still
falls short
--- the Tier-1/2 factor basis, the constant multi-year district shares, and the
categories lacking an independent district record --- is exactly where
country-specific measurement, not more sophisticated disaggregation, would have the
largest effect.
