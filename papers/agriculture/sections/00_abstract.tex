Pakistan's agricultural emissions remain poorly resolved spatially, and the country lacks a
gridded agricultural air-pollutant inventory based on national activity data. We develop a
reproducible framework that calculates greenhouse-gas and air-pollutant emissions from a
shared set of activity data and a common nitrogen accounting system. The framework combines
official Pakistani statistics, open global datasets, and published IPCC and EMEP/EEA
methods.

We construct a \emph{hybrid Tier~1/Tier~2} inventory covering six IPCC agricultural
categories and allocate the resulting emissions to provinces, a \SI{0.05}{\degree} grid, and
116 district units plus one residual unit. Activity data and emission factors determine the magnitude of
each emission stream, while process-matched spatial proxies determine its distribution. Each
spatial product is rescaled to its control total, ensuring exact reconciliation across
national, provincial, gridded, and district estimates.

For 2024, we estimate agricultural greenhouse-gas emissions of \SI{223}{}~Mt~\COe{} using
AR5 GWP-100 values, with an analytical uncertainty of $\pm$19\% and a Monte-Carlo 95\%
interval of \SI{185}{}--\SI{271}{}~Mt~\COe{}. We also estimate \SI{2.7}{}~Mt of \NHthree{}
and, from crop-residue burning, \SI{0.65}{}~Mt of CO and \SI{54}{}~kt of PM$_{2.5}$. The
air-pollutant inventory is provided on the same grid and district geography and as a
1990--2024 national time series.

We evaluate district patterns against independent government crop records and compare the
gridded ammonia surface with an independent \SI{0.1}{\degree} inventory for South Asia. The
two ammonia inventories show strong spatial agreement, with a Pearson correlation of
\num{0.90} and \SI{78}{\percent} overlap among top-decile cells. However, our national
estimate is approximately \num{1.8} times higher, with a broadly consistent ratio across
provinces. Stream-level comparisons show that aggregate statistics conceal important
differences among livestock, fertiliser, and residue-burning emissions.

The ammonia estimate is particularly sensitive to the assumed distribution of
manure-management systems, which has not been measured nationally. Across plausible
configurations, national \NHthree{} emissions range from \SI{1.7}{} to \SI{3.0}{}~Mt. We
therefore report structural and parametric uncertainty separately and treat the
livestock-ammonia estimate as provisional. The open-source framework provides a reproducible
basis for biennial transparency reporting and a nationally grounded gridded agricultural
emissions inventory for Pakistan.

The sector described here is the largest component of a ten-sector national inventory built
on the same engine, in which agriculture is \SI{50.2}{\percent} of \SI{444.96}{}~Mt~\COe{}
\citep{ahmad2026national}. Two of its boundaries are accounted for against another sector rather than
described in prose: dung-cake nitrogen leaves the manure-management balance here, and the
part the household survey observes burned arrives as household fuel in the residential
sector while the remainder is carried as an unresolved row outside the total; and the crop
residue burned as household fuel
(\SI{9.27}{}~Mt dry matter, \SI{122192}{}~TJ) leaves the residue balance here and arrives
in the same fuel ledger---an exact transfer rather than a pair of independent estimates. Readers
concerned with those transfers, with the cross-sector uncertainty treatment,
or with where agriculture sits among national key categories should read the two papers
together.
