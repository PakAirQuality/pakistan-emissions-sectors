Pakistan's official inventory reproduces the metered residential fossil core---its
gaseous activity sits within \SI{1.4}{\percent} of the regulator-derived quantity used
here, and within \SI{8}{\percent} on its own scope and year---but reports every
residential biomass cell as not estimated. We combine regulatory statistics, two rounds
of household-survey microdata and cross-sector biomass ledgers to estimate nine
household fuel streams (IPCC 1A4b), with greenhouse gases and air pollutants computed
from one activity basis. Firewood is triangulated by three mutually corroborating
estimates, but dung is not: the agriculture nitrogen ledger implies
\SI{58.74}{\mega\tonne} of air-dry cake where the household survey, interpolated
between its two rounds, puts \SI{10.93}{} in households,
a factor of \num{5.4} that mass conservation cannot adjudicate. The reference takes
household dung from the survey (configuration S), whose two rounds themselves
disagree by a factor of \num{1.53} per household; the configuration in which
households burn all the diverted dung (L) is reported as the alternative, and the
diverted dung with no observed household destination is carried explicitly as
unresolved, outside the total. Across the two configurations the sector emits
\SIrange{24.2}{29.5}{\mega\tonne} \COe{} for FY2023--24 (\SIrange{5.4}{6.5}{\percent}
of a ten-sector national inventory) and \SIrange{283}{632}{\kilo\tonne} of gross primary
\PM{}, \numrange{49}{109} times road transport's exhaust particulate, the reference
being the lower figure of each range; the dung quantity
and destination are the dominant structural uncertainty in both emission layers, and
the reference falls within \SI{15}{\percent} of the independent CCAC/SEI
assessment at the matched year where configuration~L is \num{2.6} times
above it. The results show that climate and primary-emission inventories can rank the
same sector very differently, while intervention and exposure rankings require evidence
beyond a gross-emission inventory.
