\subsection{Global gridded inventories}

EDGAR \citep{edgar2025booklet,crippa2020} is the most complete source of Pakistani sector estimates
and the principal external comparator used here. It applies a consistent global methodology
to nationally reported activity data, which is its strength for cross-country comparison and
its limitation for any single country: where a national statistic exists that EDGAR's
global pipeline does not ingest---a railway operator's metered fuel purchase, a regulator's
fuel-consumption table, a provincial production series---EDGAR necessarily models what a
national compiler can read.

FAOSTAT \citep{faostat,tubiello2013} covers agriculture and land use with a Tier~1 method
applied to FAO's own activity database. Climate TRACE \citep{climatetrace} takes a different
approach again, inferring emissions from remote sensing and asset-level data. All three
disagree with each other for Pakistan by margins that are material: their agriculture totals
span \SIrange{233}{261}{\mega\tonne}.

The disagreement is not a defect of any of them. It reflects that a global product must
choose defaults where a national compiler can consult a source, and this inventory's value
proposition rests on that difference: it is built from Pakistani statistics---the Pakistan
Statistical Year Book, NEPRA's State of Industry Report, OGRA's petroleum tables, Pakistan
Railways Year Books, provincial statistical bureaux---rather than from a global model's
Pakistani slice.

\subsection{National inventory compilation as software}

Most national inventories are compiled in spreadsheets, often in the IPCC Inventory Software
or a national derivative. The literature on this is thin, which is itself notable: the
software engineering of inventory compilation is largely undiscussed in the emissions
literature, while the substantive question---how do you know a large multi-source arithmetic
artefact is internally consistent?---is well studied in software engineering under the
heading of invariant checking.

Reproducibility infrastructure has reached adjacent fields. Emissions research increasingly
publishes code alongside results, and gridded products ship their processing chains. What is
less common is treating the \emph{cross-sector properties} as testable artefacts: the
double-counting checks, the mass balances between sectors, the notation-key bookkeeping. Those
are usually documented in a methods report and verified by review. This paper's contribution
is to argue, with a worked ten-sector instance, that they are better expressed as executable
assertions.

\subsection{Pakistani inventories}

Pakistan's First Biennial Transparency Report \citep{pakbtr2024} is the country's current
submission and publishes a full IPCC-category inventory for FY2020--21; the First Biennial
Update Report \citep{pakbur1} precedes it and reports for FY2017--18. The Global Change Impact Studies Centre has
published national inventory work \citep{gcisc}, and sectoral studies exist for brick kilns
\citep{brickkilns}, mangroves \citep{hussain2022mangrove} and crop residue burning
\citep{mir2022}. None is simultaneously current, reproducible and accompanied by per-sector
uncertainty, which is the gap this work addresses. Completeness is not the axis on which
this inventory can claim an advantage, and it would be unfair to grade others on it while
exempting ourselves: of the \num{138} detailed categories tracked here, \num{71} remain NE
and \num{8} IE, fluorinated gases and the non-gas captive fleet among them, so PakEmis is a
more reproducible inventory rather than a more complete one. Measured against the current
national submission for a matched fiscal year (Section~\ref{sec:btr}) the shortfall is
\SI{6.04}{\mega\tonne} across seven categories, and that is nearly the whole of the national
difference between the two accounts. Section~\ref{sec:completeness}
reports its own notation keys on the same terms.

Where this inventory disagrees with BUR-1 it says so and argues the case; the railways
disagreement in Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks} is by a factor of ten and is resolved on the
operator's own published fuel account.

\subsection{Uncertainty treatment}

IPCC's Volume~1 Chapter~3 defines Approach~1 error propagation and Approach~2 Monte-Carlo
simulation, and the 2019 Refinement \citep{ipcc2019} restates both. Approach~2 permits
correlated inputs; in practice, published national inventories rarely state whether shared
emission factors were correlated across sectors, and the Approach~1 formula makes
independence the default reading. Section~\ref{sec:uncertainty} quantifies what that
undeclared assumption is worth for a country with six fuel-burning categories drawing on the
same IPCC defaults.
