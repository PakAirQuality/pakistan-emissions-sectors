\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Pakistan's emission sources are represented in international inventories, but national activity, material transfers, reporting boundaries and source geography are not interchangeable with their global defaults. Public regulators and statistical agencies provide fuel, production, crop, nutrient, household and demographic records at different periods and administrative scales. Reconstructing compatible activities and documenting revisions makes those sources usable together.

PakEmis provides one greenhouse-gas and air-pollutant inventory based on that reconstruction. The common core covers July 2015--June 2025. Annual source records, compatible monthly estimates, administrative allocations, native cell masses, uncertainty and query products describe the same named configurations. The reference is S\_TAN: survey-supported household dung and a coherent total-ammoniacal-nitrogen formulation. L and the IPCC nitrogen alternative are retained separately.

The contribution is source evidence and accounting rather than a larger species list or finer nominal grid. Public-power coal combines station consumption and rank NCVs rather than treating the publisher's aggregate energy conversion as an unquestioned physical account. Industrial coal retains unresolved quantities and dated kiln alternatives. Household surveys identify supported combustion while leaving other diverted dung destinations unresolved. Nitrogen pathways link NH$_3$, NO$_x$ and N$_2$O from the same manure and soil states.

The core air panel is primary PM$_{2.5}$, BC, NO$_x$, SO$_2$, CO, NMVOC and NH$_3$. Their coverage, control assumptions and emission boundaries differ by process. Supplemental PM$_{10}$, TSP and OC are retained where supported. BC/OC are particulate components; air mass is not CO$_2$-equivalent and is not ambient concentration or exposure. No LULUCF, chemistry model or health assessment is incorporated into the core.

One national data-description article is supported by this expanded technical report and maintained sector notes. Quantitative claims here use current released products. Historical debugging and numerical revisions belong in the release change record; the following sections describe the final methods, limitations and useful comparisons.
