% ===========================================================================
\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
% ===========================================================================

\textbf{The total is a single-year national total, and two of its ten modules pay for that
with a borrowed shape.}\label{lim:single-year} All ten sectors report FY2023--24, stated as explicit dates on every
generated output. Getting there cost two declared compromises rather than a caveat: transport
takes its magnitude and its provincial shares from the reference period's own direct OCAC
editions but borrows its fleet/class composition from the registration panel's last year
(FY2022--23), and waste's district
population is projected one year past its census anchor to the fiscal midpoint. Neither moves
a national tonne---a share set sums to one---but both are weaker evidence than the quantity
they sit beside, and the component rows say so.

\textbf{Roughly \SI{47}{\mega\tonne} of this inventory's incompleteness can be given a
number, and that figure is neither a floor nor a total.}\label{lim:incomplete-floor}
Industrial fuel combustion is the largest residual, and it halved in this release: EDGAR
puts the category near \SI{44}{\mega\tonne}, and beside the \SI{5.55}{\mega\tonne} of
captive self-generation, \SI{5.61}{\mega\tonne} of industrial process gas and
\SI{3.40}{\mega\tonne} of industrial liquid fuels, the inventory now estimates
\SI{2.78}{\mega\tonne} of brick-kiln and \SI{10.80}{\mega\tonne} of cement-sector coal
from the HDIP coal-by-consumer series, leaving about \SI{15.85}{\mega\tonne} outstanding
rather than the whole category.

That \SI{15.85}{\mega\tonne} is a coverage difference against one comparator and is not a
lower bound on absent mass. Run over all nine years, the same comparison puts this
inventory \emph{above} EDGAR in four of them, by as much as \SI{6.63}{\mega\tonne} in
FY2020--21, and a quantity that changes sign cannot bound what is missing. The gap opens in
FY2022--23 and FY2023--24 alone, the two years in which this inventory's own 1.A.2 falls
\SI{25.25}{\mega\tonne} below its FY2021--22 level---brick-kiln coal, cement-sector coal and
industrial furnace oil together---while EDGAR's stays flat, so the difference prices a discontinuity in our activity
series at least as much as it prices categories nobody estimated. EDGAR is in any case
IEA-balance-derived and fed upstream by the same HDIP record this inventory reads, which
makes it a diagnostic rather than an independent measurement of what we are missing.

F-gases (\SI{11.54}{\mega\tonne}) are absent entirely. Both figures come from an external
estimate, which is what lets them be written down at all, but they are not the same kind of
quantity: the F-gas figure prices a category this inventory does not estimate, while the
\SI{15.85}{\mega\tonne} is a difference between two estimates of a category both inventories
estimate. The rest cannot be valued even that far. Chemical IPPU---ammonia, nitric acid, soda ash, lime, iron
and steel---has no independent estimate here at all, and Pakistan's own filing puts the
chemical industries near \SI{19.4}{\mega\tonne}; industrial wastewater, waste incineration and
biological treatment are likewise unquantified, as is mobile air-conditioning. Commercial and
institutional combustion (1.A.4.a)---absent from this register altogether until an audit of
the commercial-class rows the repository already held---is now declared and screened at
roughly \SI{2.4}{\mega\tonne} across its two quantifiable streams; domestic navigation and
the coal-mining surface share carry published screening estimates outside the total.
\emph{The \SI{47}{\mega\tonne} is therefore what can be counted, not what is
missing.} Section~\ref{sec:completeness} names all of them. This is a statement about coverage, not accuracy,
and it means the national figure should be read as ``ten sectors totalling
\SI{445}{\mega\tonne} under reference scenario \texttt{REF\_2023\_24}'' rather than as Pakistan's emissions. One named
structural case crosses sectors: the residential dung stream is reported there under two
configurations, the household-survey reading and the agriculture-ledger supply
derivation, which diverts \num{4.9} times what the 2024--25 survey round measured. This rollup carries the survey reading, which is
the reference. The diverted dung it does not burn in households, \SI{47.81}{\mega\tonne} of
air-dry cake, is unresolved and outside the total, and configuration~L, the destination
assumption that households burn all of it, would raise the \texttt{REF\_2023\_24} total by
\SI{5.25}{\mega\tonne}.

\textbf{LULUCF enters no total, on either side.}\label{lim:lulucf} No land-use, land-use-change or forestry
flux enters the inventory total or any benchmark comparison. An exploratory Tier~1 land
module ships with the release and publishes, outside every total, a provisional net sink
of \SI{-4.01}{\mega\tonne} \COe{} (forest land \SI{-3.52}{} and Indus-delta mangrove
blue carbon \SI{-0.49}{}; cropland and grassland soil carbon not estimated) in
\texttt{outputs/lulucf\_totals.csv}, discussed in Supplement~S1 of the agriculture paper.
For a country with substantial mangrove and rangeland carbon the gap in the reported
inventory is real, and it is a different kind of gap from the others: LULUCF can be a sink,
so its absence does not simply bias the total low.

That provisional sink rests on an assumption Pakistan's own submission contradicts, and the
disagreement is now priced in
\texttt{outputs/pakemis\_forest\_flux\_adjudication.csv}. The module sets deforestation to
\num{0}~ha a year, justified by FAO's net forest conversion being about zero in recent years;
Pakistan's Common Reporting Tables put forest-land gross losses at \SI{14868.348}{} against
gross gains of \SI{7113.083}{} kilotonnes of carbon, a ratio of \num{2.09} that nets to
\SI{28.436}{\mega\tonne} of CO\textsubscript{2} and reproduces the submission's own printed
figure. So the two accounts that read forest land as a \emph{sink} are not two: the
exploratory module takes the decisive quantity from FAOSTAT, whose net forest conversion is
exactly \num{0.000}~kha from 2020 against \num{26.104}~kha in 2015 while its
Pakistani forest area rises at exactly \SI{20.786}{} kha a year---a step constant to three
decimals over four years, which is interpolation between Forest Resources Assessment anchors
and not an annual observation. The gap between the submission and FAOSTAT is
\SI{32.55}{\mega\tonne} of CO\textsubscript{2} and it is a disagreement about direction, so
no forest component can be built from either until the area trend is settled. The parameter
is left at zero rather than changed, because moving it moves this printed figure and that is
a declared decision rather than an audit edit.

\textbf{The fugitive estimate is conditional on technology and coverage, and the
reference sits high in its own envelope.}\label{lim:fugitive-envelope} The 2019 segment
ledger replaces the capped 2006 range treatment and gives a parametric interval conditional
on higher/default practices. Lower-practice, throughput-basis, incomplete-coverage and
leak-base membership
configurations form a separate structural envelope of \SIrange{9.601}{24.694}{\mega\tonne},
and the reference (\SI{23.499}{\mega\tonne}) sits one membership step below the envelope's
\emph{top}---conservative default-practice factors with full coverage, and fertiliser
feedstock outside the post-meter leakage base---not at an
evidential centre. The top is the feedstock-inside membership of every release through
rc66, retained and not defended. The envelope now ships as a named configuration family
(\texttt{fugitives\_structural\_envelope}: the reference, the lower-practice member at
\SI{14.894}{\mega\tonne}, and the joint floor at \SI{9.601}{\mega\tonne}) with its
selection rule declared, so a national quote of the reference never travels without the
envelope beside it. No Pakistani component-measurement
campaign yet selects among those configurations.

\textbf{The series is complete, and its residual weaknesses are in basis rather than
coverage.}\label{lim:series-basis} All ten sectors are reconstructed consistently across FY2015--16 to
FY2023--24: the trend-safe \textsc{harmonized common scope} view and the full-scope view
coincide since the FY2015--16 product splits were recovered from the public record
(Section~\ref{sec:series}). The scope caveat that used to sit here---differencing across
the first year measured sectors entering the record---is retired with its premise. What
the completeness of the RECTANGLE does not change is the basis beneath it. Agriculture
sits inside the constant scope on a \emph{fiscal} reconstruction, and what remains outside
that reconstruction is narrower than what used to. Earlier releases ran every pre-anchor
year on FAOSTAT calendar-year activity---crops, fertiliser and livestock stocks alike---and
called the result a hybrid diagnostic series rather than a trend-safe one. That description
is superseded: crop and nitrogen activity now comes from the Economic Survey's own
fiscal-year tables in all nine years, the series marks agriculture
\texttt{reconstructed-fiscal}, and rebuilding it corrected a misalignment of a full calendar
year in three of the four crops the old history carried (Section~\ref{sec:agri-basis}).
\textbf{One half of the sector is still not on a fiscal basis, and it is the larger half.}
Enteric fermentation and manure management---\SI{77.37}{\percent} of agriculture's
reference-year mass---are driven by livestock stocks, and Pakistan publishes no fiscal-year
\emph{enumeration} of the national herd: the Economic Survey's fiscal head counts are, by
their own footnote, the inter-census projection FAOSTAT already carries, so adopting them
would buy a label and no observation. Those two categories keep a calendar driver and an
inter-census projection rather than an enumeration, and the six-month phase error that
leaves is measured at under \SI{2}{\percent} of each category in every year rather than
asserted. The step is priced rather than smoothed. The nine-year ten-sector panel is complete:
the FY2015--16 product splits for road transport and industrial combustion---its last
absent cells---were recovered at rc20, and no sector-year in the panel is NE. It never
did require more clinker trade returns: the monthly HS~252310 panel now runs July 2014 to June 2024 for both flows, and
every year of the series resolves to an observed trade term---which is what opened
FY2021--22 and FY2022--23, the two years that also held activity; the consequence for
key-category analysis is in Section~\ref{sec:keycat}. The
underlying cause is partly source availability and partly source \emph{format}: five Pakistan
Railways Year Book editions are scanned images with no extractable text layer. FY2018 and
FY2019 were missing from the railway series for that reason and are not any more---both were
recovered from the historical tables a later readable edition reprints, and the series is
unbroken from FY2014 to FY2025.

\textbf{Several partitions are exhaustive but soft at the split point.}\label{lim:soft-partitions} Section~\ref{sec:closures}
guarantees that the parts sum to the whole; it does not guarantee the split. The waste
collection rate of \SI{50.3}{\percent} moves emissions between two categories with different
methane yields. The off-road machinery class inherits its source workbook's residual
bucket---the catch-all ``Other vehicles'' class maps into it wholesale and is then modelled
as \SI{100}{\percent} diesel---so the carve-out's magnitude is sound while its composition is
not, and the cropland proxy that allocates it provincially is right for the tractors and
wrong for the remainder. The transport sector's vehicle-class split is declared wrong for the
same reason and is not used for any reported number that depends on it.

\textbf{Household firewood is no longer the largest single activity uncertainty, and the
reason is worth recording.}\label{lim:firewood} It is the largest household fuel by energy---
\SI{38.7}{\percent} of household input, against dung cake's \SI{13.6}{\percent}---and this
inventory used to carry it at \SI{60}{\percent} activity uncertainty because the only
national quantity available was FAOSTAT's, which carries FAO's own estimate flag in every
year since 2009 and does not move.

It is now a measurement. The HIES 2024--25 round gives a design-weighted national household
firewood quantity, and two independent anchors bracket it: the 2018--19 round reproduces the
2024--25 total to within six percent, and FAOSTAT's own series, converted on a
moisture-matched basis rather than the oven-dry-with-as-received pairing that had been
compared against it, sits seven percent above. The stream is therefore reported at
\SI{35}{\percent}, which carries the survey's stratum-clustered sampling error plus the
systematic risks a household survey cannot see---one-month consumption recall, out-of-home
and informal-enterprise use booked to households, and seasonal enumeration.

FAOSTAT has not been discarded; it has been demoted from anchor to named structural case,
\texttt{firewood\_faostat}, which puts the sector at \SI{23.89}{\mega\tonne} against the
reported \SI{24.22}{\mega\tonne}. Dropping firewood entirely gives \SI{20.81}{\mega\tonne}.
Both are published on every run, because an excluded number is invisible in a way that a
flagged one is not, and because a reader is entitled to see what the re-anchoring did rather
than only its result.

\textbf{Air pollutants are partial, but less partial than this section used to claim.}\label{lim:air-pollutants} Seven
sectors---agriculture, residential, transport, waste, power, railways and aviation---now build
an air-pollutant layer and write machine-readable outputs. A previous edition recorded
power-sector and residential-gas pollutants as not estimated, blocked on EMEP/EEA factors the
agency no longer serves in a retrievable form, and that diagnosis was wrong in both cases:
the residential sector holds the small-combustion chapter and reports gas, LPG, kerosene and
coal, and the power sector reads AP-42 directly, including a fuel sulphur content that
published Thar analyses do bound. What remains genuinely not estimated is narrower and
sharper: what the power fleet \emph{actually} emits, since no national control-device
inventory has been obtained here and the reported figures are uncontrolled upper bounds; the
equipment mix behind industrial combustion, whose boiler, furnace, engine and turbine shares
are declared rather than surveyed and are worth a factor of thirty on diesel \NOx{}; within
the IPPU and fugitive layers, the classes no admissible factor or series can price---urea
finishing, nitric acid, lime, glass, venting, post-meter losses---each typed absent with its
activity written beside it; and the transport
processes---cold start, evaporative NMVOC, non-exhaust particulate---named in that sector's
own register. The completeness table is the authority on which is which.

\textbf{Emission factors are overwhelmingly Tier~1 defaults.}\label{lim:tier1} Apart from the agriculture
sector's Tier~2 elements, documented separately \citep{ahmad2026ag}, this inventory applies
IPCC defaults to Pakistani activity data. That is the appropriate tier given the available
information, and it means the estimates carry the defaults' assumptions about technology
mix, combustion conditions and management practice---assumptions derived largely from
countries whose infrastructure differs from Pakistan's.

\textbf{The monthly layer rests on physics more than on observation, and on neither for a
third of it.}\label{lim:monthly-physics} Every source stream is resolved into twelve months and every month row declares
how. Stating that as one number---\SI{54.65}{\percent} of the reference year on a flat
profile---is true and misleading, because it does not distinguish a flat month that is the
physical answer from a flat month that is nobody having read the records. Across the nine-year
product the honest statement is three figures, generated from the released rows in
\texttt{outputs/pakemis\_temporal\_decomposition.csv}: \SI{37.3}{\percent} of monthly mass has
its within-year variation carried by an observed monthly series; \SI{41.0}{\percent} is
continuous or stock-driven, where flat---properly, days-weighted---is the correct answer and
acquiring a monthly series would not change the shape; and \SI{21.8}{\percent} genuinely
varies within the year and is carried flat because no monthly evidence was obtained.
These three figures and the TE-class flat share are different classifications of the same
mass---TE4 contains both physically appropriate flatness and unobserved variability---and
the exact bridge between them is published as
\texttt{outputs/pakemis\_flatness\_crosswalk.csv}, joined at the month-row grain the
coverage summary grades, so it reproduces the TE-class flat share exactly and carries no
grain difference.

\textbf{The historical monthly product is graded below A$-$, explicitly.}\label{lim:monthly-grade} The release sets
itself a gate for the monthly layer---no more than \SI{15}{\percent} of nine-year monthly mass
may be time-variable and carried flat for an A$-$---and the nine-year product does not meet
it: \SI{21.76}{\percent} of its mass varies within the year and is carried on the calendar
alone. The reference-year product is a different object and is stated separately: FY2023--24
alone carries \SI{9.57}{\percent}, inside the gate. So the grade is a statement about the
eight historical years, not about the reference year, and it is a row of the exit scorecard
(\texttt{outputs/pakemis\_pass2\_scorecard.csv}, \texttt{2b-historical-temporal-grade})
rather than a sentence alone. One acquisition decides it: road transport's months before July
2021 are flat because OCAC's monthly editions before that date are not held, and the
pre-July-2021 editions requested under \texttt{MON-OCAC-01} carry \SI{283.2}{\mega\tonne} of
nine-year mass; with them the figure would be \SI{14.68}{\percent}, inside the gate.

That gap is a property of the source rather than of the effort spent on it, which is worth
stating plainly because the sentence above would otherwise read as a download nobody has got
round to. OCAC's own archive lists monthly sectoral-sales editions from the current month back
to July 2021 and stops; before that date it publishes fiscal-year compilations only, back to
FY2016-17, and this inventory already holds those as its annual controls. The monthly series
does not exist in public form for the six years at issue, so no acquisition of a published
file closes the gap---only a request to the compiler does. That request is drafted and unsent,
and an arrival is an ingest for a later release, not a promise made by this one.

The largest acquisition of this release moves less than it should, and where it stops is the
finding. NFDC's consolidated volume publishes fertiliser offtake by product and month for
FY2012-13 to FY2022-23 on this inventory's own July--June fiscal year, so the eight years before
the reference period need no borrowed months at all; the parse is gated on the three controls
the table prints for itself---each row's total, each product's \texttt{TOTAL} row, and the page
grand total---and validated against the independently transcribed Annex-7 at their three-month
overlap, where the two agree to two tonnes on four hundred thousand
(\texttt{outputs/nfdc\_monthly\_parse\_summary.csv},
\texttt{nfdc\_monthly\_quarantine.csv}). Urea application reaches \SI{100}{\percent} observed.
Managed soils then moved for a reason that had nothing to do with acquisition. The offtake
shape may only ride the synthetic-fertiliser share, and the atomic annual table appeared to
split managed soils into subitems for the reference year alone. It never lacked the split:
\texttt{fiscal\_series} had been computing all thirty-five components for every year and
discarding them on the way out, while the annual product returned nothing for those years and
recorded in its own documentation that agriculture's history was ``carried by category
and gas, not by stream''---a sentence true of the FAOSTAT reconstruction that preceded the
fiscal seam and false of the engine that replaced it. Removing that declared absence let the
offtake shape reach the synthetic share in all nine years: \SI{95.09}{\mega\tonne} moved from
unobserved to observed, no annual number changed, and the re-aggregated subitems reproduce the
published category totals across all \num{72} year-category-gas cells to
\SI{5e-7}{\mega\tonne}, which is the series' own rounding. \textbf{The deposited and applied
census nitrogen---pasture, organic manure, crop residues---stayed flat}, because it rides a
livestock count and a harvest rather than a dealer's depot, and a test asserts it did not come
along.

The PBS minerals panel, parsed two releases ago and deliberately left unread because each
canonical mineral resolves in only five or six of the eight held editions, is now spent---per
source-year, as review ruled it should be: observed years carry the year's own twelve months at
TE2, missing years are days-weighted TE4, and no year wears another's shape. The upstream
fugitive streams---gas production, gathering, processing and sour-gas processing on the
monthly gas series, oil production on crude---moved \SI{93.66}{\mega\tonne} of cumulative
nine-year mass from unobserved to observed timing, \SI{43.0}{\percent} of the sector in the
reference year, without any annual figure moving; eight of nine years now carry their own
months for both streams, three of them recovered from parse defects inside editions the
repository already held. The demand-side streams---distribution,
transmission, post-meter, LNG---deliberately did not: deliveries and send-out have their own
season that field output does not observe, and a production shape on them would be the
wrong-driver overclaim; they stay flat with the acquisition named
(\texttt{outputs/pbs\_minerals\_parse\_summary.csv} carries the per-mineral coverage,
\texttt{pbs\_minerals\_quarantine.csv} the rows the source's own totals reject, and a test
holds the production shape to the upstream streams alone).

``Observed'' in that sentence means the panel's own twelve months, and for part of the
panel it does not mean the country. \texttt{outputs/pakemis\_minerals\_panel\_screening.csv}
reports which province-years are whole: of \num{28} mineral-years, \num{13} are served as
covered from an incomplete set of provinces, because the coverage test asks for twelve
months rather than for four provinces. The consequence is bounded and it is not uniform.
For natural gas it is small---Pakistani production has almost no season and the provinces'
curves are alike, so recomputing a complete year's shape from only the provinces a broken
year holds moves a monthly share by about a percentage point. For crude oil in FY2021--22
it is not small: the panel holds Balochistan alone, and the profile served for that year
puts \SI{78.76}{\percent} of the year in a single month and labels it observed. No real
month of Pakistani crude production is most of the year.

The provinces are not missing from the source, and that is the part worth stating
plainly. PBS prints all four. The mineral's name cell is vertically centred across its
five province rows, so a layout-preserving text extraction places the name on the
\emph{middle} line, and a parser that treats the name line as the start of the group loses
the rows printed above it. The arithmetic identifies them beyond argument: for coal in
FY2021--22 the Punjab and Sindh rows printed above the name line, taken with the KPK and
Balochistan rows on and below it, equal the edition's printed total in every one of the
twelve months exactly. The panel carries \SI{42.1}{\percent} of that year's coal.

Worse, the parse's own assurance cannot see this. It reports how many mineral-years close
their group control, and that control compares the rows the parser held in hand against
the printed total---not the rows the panel ships. For coal in FY2021--22 it closes to the
unit while certifying a quantity the panel does not contain. \num{9} canonical mineral-years
disagree between a closing control and the shipped panel, in both directions: limestone
FY2016--17 ships \SI{6.9}{\percent} of what its control counted, and natural gas
FY2020--21 ships \SI{230.8}{\percent} of it, that one being the worksheet duplication
described next. A control that passes while its subject is short is not a control, and the
comparison it omitted is now computed and declared.

The same screening separates a second defect that points the other way. The spreadsheet
editions are read across every worksheet, and some workbooks repeat their table on working
sheets, so those years are counted once per sheet---FY2020--21 carries \num{31} rows in
every month for gas and oil and \num{100} for coal and limestone, and its published gas
quantity is \num{12.83} times the last year the panel records correctly. That is a wrong number in a
shipped activity table and it costs the inventory nothing, because the duplication is
uniform across the twelve months and every consumer normalises; the missing provinces cost
the inventory something and leave the quantity looking plausible. Neither is repaired here:
the first would move a published activity table and the second a published monthly shape,
and both are decisions rather than audit edits. They are counted so the decision can be
taken on a measurement.

\textbf{Only that last third is a gap}\label{lim:monthly-gap}, and it is the only one of the three that acquiring
data would close. A livestock census counts a stock once and carries no month, so equal
twelfths---days-weighted---is the complete statement the activity data supports, and the layer
is built so that such a row can never receive a shaped profile. The rest is a gap with a name. Road CNG is rationed by season and by load-shedding and is spread flat because
no monthly metered series was obtained. Sugar-mill bagasse burns in a crushing season this
release does not assemble. Crop-residue burning rode that same sentence for three
releases and no longer belongs in it: the nine-year VIIRS fire record now times it
(TE3, every month observed within the attribution ladder's terms). Waste's disposal
stream likewise left the flat set---the continuous FOD state engine computes its
months from the deposit history---while the sector's other streams stay flat with
their reasons named. Fugitives
is flat only on its demand side: the upstream production, gathering and processing streams
ride PBS's monthly gas and crude series where a year holds them (\SI{43.0}{\percent} of
the sector's reference year at TE2), while distribution, transmission, LNG and post-meter
streams stay deliberately flat, because field production is not a defensible timing proxy
for downstream deliveries.
None of this moves the annual total by construction, and all of it would move the months.
The single largest improvement available to the temporal dimension is acquisition---OGRA's
monthly gas system reports, a crushing-season series, provincial harvest dates---rather than
a better model of what is already held.

\textbf{The archive deposit contains products, not the source documents.}\label{lim:deposit} The inventory reads
\num{217} registered sources, of which \num{153} are third-party publications archived in the
repository---OGRA, NEPRA, PBS, NFDC and PCAA reports, and PBS household survey microdata.
\emph{None of them is in the deposit.} For \num{126} of those \num{217} registered sources the
publisher states no redistribution terms at all, which is the ordinary condition of a
government statistical publication and not a defect in the record; but a deposit is a
publication, and this project cannot license what it does not own. Depositing the repository
whole would have offered the \num{60} distinct third-party documents it holds under a
CC~BY grant it had no right to make.

What ships instead is \texttt{outputs/pakemis\_source\_register.csv}: every source named, its
publisher and edition recorded, its archive hashed, and the instruction for obtaining it from
that publisher. The two questions the register keeps apart are the reason this works---whether
a document may be \emph{read and derived from} is not the question of whether it may be
\emph{republished}, and only the second is at issue here. The derived quantities are facts and
this project's compilations of them, and those are deposited: \num{1761} files under
licences the project can actually grant, each hashed in
\texttt{outputs/pakemis\_deposit\_manifest.csv} with the licence it ships under.

One file cannot have a single licence, and saying that it did was a real error rather than
an imprecision. The facility registry carries a licence column qualifying each of its
\num{12680} rows, and \num{71} of them are OpenStreetMap derivatives under ODbL~1.0---a
share-alike position this project cannot waive, and the same position the paragraph above
asserts it cannot assume for anything it does not own. Both the register and its published
twin were nevertheless typed \texttt{own-work} in the deposit manifest, with the reason
``this project's own code, product or prose'', so the archive would have offered those
\num{71} database rows under a CC~BY grant. They now ship as \texttt{mixed-per-row} with
the per-row licence counts in the reason, which is the only honest whole-file statement
about a file whose rows disagree. The distinction that keeps this a narrow repair rather
than a sweeping rule is that two other shipped registers also carry non-uniform licence
columns, and theirs describe \emph{other} artefacts: a register's judgement about a
third-party document is this project's own work however that document is licensed. Which
of the two a file is, is now a declared list on both sides rather than a default.

The ODbL carve-outs are also named twice now, by published identity and by the path the
archive actually ships them at, because the two differ after the data-layout migration and
a reader searching the deposit for the published identity alone found nothing---from which
they could reasonably have concluded the files were excluded. They are not excluded; they
ship, correctly typed, under ODbL.

A citation names a document; a reader needs the table. The standing objection to this
release is the finer one---given the report, where exactly is the number?---and until now
nothing counted the answer.
\texttt{outputs/pakemis\_citation\_pointer\_audit.csv} does, and the denominator is most
of the work. A registered source that was \emph{derived} inside this repository has a
derivation chain rather than a page number, and a source that is a dataset rather than a
document has a file and a variable rather than a table; scoring either against a table
pointer would inflate the gap with rows no citation could close. Of the \num{131} sources
where a table pointer is the right notion, \num{115} have one. A pointer counts whether it
is in the register row or in the produced table's own per-row source column, which is
where this project's hand-verified ingests put it---checking only the first is how the
same gap previously read as \num{157} rather than \num{16}. And \emph{pointed} is not the
same as \emph{checkable}: \num{103} of the \num{112} point into a document this repository
archives, where a reader can open it and confirm the figure, while \num{7} point into
documents the project does not hold and \num{5} into files derived inside it. The
distinction changes no verdict---a pointer into a document the reader must obtain is still
a pointer---but the audit's strongest number should not imply more than it has. One pointer
was checked to the cell while that column was added: the bagasse generation ingest against
NEPRA's held State of Industry Report, where a plant reads
\SIlist{15.10;29.83;17.38;25.02;29.10}{\giga\watt\hour} in the source and the same five
values in the ingest.

Both halves are ratcheted, in the same direction, because the easiest way to improve this
share is not to cite anything but to widen the exemption. The \num{16} that remain are a
long tail of one-offs rather than a systematic failure, and the count is deliberately
conservative: a handful of dataset sources whose honest pointer is a file and a row filter
are scored as unpointed because the audit's publisher markers do not recognise them, and
widening those markers would improve the share by exemption rather than by citation. Three
named groups were closed
while measuring. The seven OCAC extractions had no pointer anywhere, and the documents
were held all along---each annual edition is two pages and each monthly edition eighteen,
nine areas of energy products followed by the same nine of non-energy. Their table titles
also drift, from ``Sectoral Sales / Consumption of POL Products'' through FY2019--20 to a
split pair from FY2020--21 onward, which is recorded with the pointers because an edition
whose vocabulary changed is exactly where a reader following a citation would go wrong.
The Pakistani BTR row was a third and cost nothing at all, since the document was
already open for the comparator work. The PBS minerals family was the other: four of its eight editions are PDFs carrying one
running-head table over one to ten pages, and four are workbooks with no table title at
all, where the \emph{sheet} is the pointer---and one of those, FY2020--21, carries an
``Electricity generation'' sheet beside ``mineral production'', which is why two different
parses read the same workbook for two different tables.
The acceptance note that prompted this named HDIP among the publishers lacking pointers;
every HDIP row carries one, and did before the audit was written.

``Deposited'' is not the same set as ``cited'', and this release measures the difference
rather than asserting it away.
\texttt{outputs/pakemis\_citation\_deposit\_audit.csv} takes every output file the
manuscripts name---\num{352} of them---and asks whether the archive contains it.
\num{181} are deposited and \num{171} are not.

That denominator was \num{111} before the notation repair described below, and the
correction is a lesson about measurement rather than about the archive. The manuscripts name a file in two notations: the
national sections and agriculture's appendix write the full path, under \texttt{outputs/},
while eight sector appendices write the bare filename alone in an itemised list---the same
file, typeset two ways. The audit matched the first notation alone, which reaches \num{132}
of the \num{352} files the manuscripts name; the other \num{220} are named only as a bare
filename, and of those \num{102} are in the archive and \num{118} are not. The register's two halves had been disagreeing about the word
\emph{cited} without either being wrong: the artifact inventory counts a bare filename,
because it searches for basenames, which is why those files carry the evidence role at all,
and this audit did not count it as a citation. A reader cannot obtain a file any better for
its name having been typeset with a directory in front of it. The ratchet is re-based on the
wider set and the pre-repair subset keeps its own ceiling, so widening the measurement cannot
hide a regression in the part that was already measured.

The repair also settled a question the blindness had been answering by default. When the
bare notation came into view it exposed \num{22} \emph{sector headline} products---a sector's
own totals, stream split, provincial table, monthly layer and uncertainty summary---named in
that sector's own paper and absent from the archive, which this project's own test calls
indefensible at that tier. A further \num{22} were the same gap seen from the other side:
fugitives' and ippu's appendices described their headline tables by function and named none of
them; aviation's five appeared in neither of its appendix's lists at all; and industry, the one
covered sector with no paper of its own, has its four named in this paper. All \num{44} are
named and deposited in this release, and that tier now measures zero. What remains undeposited is \num{168} files typed
\emph{diagnostic}, two typed \emph{intermediate} and one evaluation-lane table, where the
choice per file is a real one. The deposit ships a curated list and the
papers cite whichever table actually carries the number, and until the round that built this
audit nothing compared the two. Every absent file exists in the repository, so no citation points at
nothing; what they point at is something a reader holding only the archive cannot open.
Most are typed \emph{intermediate} or \emph{diagnostic}---working files no reader-facing
claim was supposed to rest on---which is the interesting part, because a results section
citing a table is a reader-facing claim resting on it. For those the role and the citation
disagree about what the file is, and the fix is one of two things per file: ship it, or cite
the deposited product carrying the same numbers. Seen from the register's own side the
disagreement is wider than the deposit question, and tracing it changed the register itself.
\num{11} files are now typed as working files on which \emph{no reader-facing claim rests}
while being named in a manuscript, \num{8} of them in this one. That count was \num{84}
before this release, and the way it fell is the substance.

The register has always required a canonical product to be cited; it never required a cited
file not to be called a working file, and that asymmetry is what let the set grow. Asking the
question in the other direction needed two repairs.

The first was to the measurement, not the tree. The scan that decides whether a manuscript
names a file read the national paper's sections, every sector paper's sections, the README and
the documentation---and not the sector \emph{supplements}' own bodies, four of which are only
partly assembled from section files. \num{17} artifacts were recorded as named by nobody while
a supplement named them, among them every figure in the aviation supplement and two regulatory
cell audits that exist precisely to be the evidence behind a claim. Repairing the scan
\emph{raised} this count, from \num{77} to \num{84}, without a single citation being added:
the figure earlier releases printed was an under-count of a set that was always larger.
Run again against the present tree, the same reconstruction finds \num{12} rather than
\num{17}, and the difference is instructive: a long filename in a monospace label needs
typesetting break hints to stay inside the page, and the scan that reads a printed name has to
strip them or it does not recognise the name it is looking for. Five of the seventeen have
since been named in a section as well, with break hints, and one normalisation was silently
blind to them---so the printed \num{17} is what the repair found in the tree of the day, and
the \num{12} is what the instrument reports now that every reader of a printed name shares
one normalisation.

The second repair was to what the register does when it has no answer. Every one of the files
in this set acquired its role from the register's final catch-all pattern rather than from a
decision, and that pattern declares a working file on which no reader-facing claim rests---so
the role a file received when nobody had classified it carried a substantive claim about it.
More patterns were the obvious fix and the wrong one: these files fall through precisely
because their names share no structure a pattern can reach, and a cover validated against the
whole inventory needed \num{61} patterns for \num{74} files. The catch-all now defers to the
evidence instead. A file nothing else classifies, which a manuscript names, is evidence behind
a claim; one nothing names is a working file. \num{74} files moved on that one change, with no
new pattern to go stale.

The part no re-typing could reach closed last, and it closed completely. A claim in the
verification registry reads a product by name and this paper prints the figure computed from
it, so a file a claim reads is a file a reader-facing claim rests on, by construction---which
is the one thing the working-file role promises is not true of it. That contradiction stood at
\num{83} products when it was first measured. The count of products a registered claim reads
while the register types them a working file is now \num{0}, and the subset named in no
manuscript at all is \num{0} with it. None of the residue was closed by re-typing, because
the evidence role requires the file to be named somewhere: each was instead named in the
sentence that already discussed it---the livestock nitrogen stage-flow ledger, its
IPCC-against-EMEP stage comparison and the air-pollutant Monte Carlo intervals in the
agriculture appendix, the national uncertainty decomposition beside its component-row count,
the waste release assignment beside the candidate list it was always printed with, the two
compressed-gas cross-check tables inside the clause that invoked their epistemic class, the
industry captive screening beside the captive figure it produced, and the delivered-gas
closure variant beside the ledger it prices. Both gates are exact now rather than ratchets, so
a single new printed figure whose evidence a reader cannot reach fails the build.

Following the same thread into the archive found the sharper version of it. A deposit-manifest
product row whose release tier is \emph{build} was skipped from the data archive under the
rule that no build artifact is deposited, while the builder's own description promised every
product row---two selections with no criterion in common, since the manifest chooses products
by name pattern and never consults the role. \num{13} hashed products sat in that gap,
essentially the whole air-pollutant family including the district panel and the
1990--2024 series. Nine are resolved by the two repairs above and by typing three
of them from the manifest's own literal naming; the remaining four are declared exclusions
with their reasons, because for those the manifest's claim may be the incidental one and which
register is right is a question about what the archive is for. The test that should have caught
all of this was named for a partition and asserted one inclusion of the two. Four were closed here in both ways---three
tables with no deposited equivalent added to the shipped set, and one monthly table
re-pointed at the nine-year product of which it is a single year's slice---and the remainder
is held under a ratchet that may only fall.

The cost is real and belongs here rather than in a footnote. A reader who wants to rebuild
this inventory from nothing must re-acquire those publications themselves. The hashes
are what make that a procedure rather than a guess: obtain the document, compare the digest,
and you can be certain you are reading what this release read. Everything else needed---the
engine, the derived activity tables, the products, the manuscripts---is in the deposit.

\subsection{A version string is not a pin, and the spatial axis had no fingerprint}

The release machinery described above protects a release's \emph{identity}: the manifest
hashes every product and the release cover prints the digest, so a reader can tell whether
the artefacts in hand are the artefacts that were built. What it did not protect is the
claim that a version \emph{label} identifies a particular content, and one case shows why
that distinction matters.

The API that serves these products fetches a release directory and verifies it against
that directory's own semantic contract, so a served copy is always internally consistent.
A provincial query answered below this repository's own figure for the same province and
year, and the cause was neither side's arithmetic: the served
release was byte-identical to the commit it was fetched from, and this repository's tree,
still carrying the same version string, had moved past it. The two trees agree on
\emph{every} national total to nine decimal places---the reported figure for the reference
year is identical on both sides---while the provincial allocation differs. The change was
a deliberate re-base giving furnace oil its own product geography, which eliminated a
residual province and redistributed its mass; it conserved the national total at every
period, exactly as intended.

That is the structural point, and it generalises beyond this one release and beyond
geography. Every guard this project has on a release's numbers is a guard on a
\emph{period total}: the manifest's national figures, the pinned configurations, the
claim matrix, the registered manuscript claims, the query goldens. A change that conserves
the period total passes all of them, so an allocation \emph{within} those totals could
move without any check noticing---and two did. The provincial case above is one. The other
is temporal: a set of cells in the national monthly table, all of them electricity and
heat production, moved within fiscal years at exactly zero net, so the annual table's
fingerprint is identical to the published vintage's while the within-year shape of power
generation is not.

It is now measured.
\texttt{data/catalog/published\_release\_vintages.csv} records each published release as a
commit and two digests, every row re-hashed out of this repository's history, and
\texttt{outputs/pakemis\_release\_axis\_fingerprints.csv} carries, for each disaggregated
table and each vintage, a coarse digest over period totals and a fine digest over
period-by-axis totals---the axis being a geography for the spatial tables and the month
for the monthly one. Of the \num{6} disaggregated tables fingerprinted, \num{0} currently
show a moved fine fingerprint under an unmoved coarse one. The other two are controls: the
air-pollutant provincial layer moved on both axes, which is precisely why the existing
guards did register that change, and the national annual table---the grain those guards
actually watch---is unmoved. Neither file alters a number, and a label whose content has
moved is now a row rather than a discovery. The magnitudes of both divergences are in
those two builders' own documentation rather than here, because each is measured against
an externally served copy this repository does not hold---and a figure a reader cannot
recompute from the archive does not belong in a sentence that the archive is supposed to
support.
