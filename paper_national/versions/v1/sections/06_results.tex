\subsection{One reference period}

All ten sectors report the same twelve months: FY2023--24, July 2023 to June 2024. Every
sector declares that period as explicit dates rather than as an integer year, and every
generated output carries them, so a reader meeting a CSV without this manuscript can see
which twelve months a row describes.

Writing the dates down was itself the finding. The integer year carried three incompatible
meanings across the inventory---the fiscal year a sector's period opened in, the one it
closed in, and a calendar year---with nothing enforcing any of them, and the collision had
already put a contradiction into an earlier edition of this paper about which year the power
sector reported. It exposed three sectors adrift, one of which nobody suspected.

Transport was reporting FY2021--22 because that was the year its provincial fuel-sales panel
was complete for, which put the national total two years behind eight other sectors in order
to protect an allocation the total does not depend on. It now takes its magnitude
\emph{and} its provincial shares from the reference year's own twelve direct OCAC area
pages---nothing provincial is borrowed since those editions were ingested---while its
fleet/class \emph{composition} still comes from the registration panel's last year
(FY2022--23), declared as borrowed and bracketed: a
share set sums to one, so a borrowed shape moves emissions between classes and cannot
change a total. The same publication's \emph{industry} pages had been sitting unread in the
same file. Industrial liquids had one provincial vector for six products, and it gave furnace
oil the retail geography of fuels it does not burn---Punjab \num{0.657} against furnace oil's
own \num{0.784}---while industrial diesel, which is very nearly half Sindh, was pulled the
other way. The two largest liquids now carry their own product's area pages, with the four
small ones blended because their shares swing on a few thousand tonnes; the pages also supply
a year axis for the two periods before the reference year. The national total is unchanged. Waste was reporting the calendar year of its census anchor; its district
population panel is now projected one year to the fiscal midpoint at the panel's own
intercensal growth rate---an extrapolation where every other year is an interpolation, and
its rows carry evidence class E3 to say so.

Agriculture, at half the inventory, was a fiscal year \emph{ahead}: its crop quantities were
the Pakistan Economic Survey 2024--25 tables and its nitrogen offtake the NFDC Review
2024--25. Both now read the 2023--24 editions. \SI{77.37}{\percent} of the sector---enteric
fermentation and manure management---rides a livestock census count rather than a flow over
any period, which the component rows record as an enumeration rather than a span, and which
is why those two categories did not move when the rest of the sector did. The Economic Survey
publishes livestock estimates for the same year; this inventory does not use them, because a
count is evidence and a projection is not.

\textbf{Closing that offset exposed something larger than the offset.} The two publications
agriculture cites were not in this project's source archive at all---nor was any Economic
Survey or NFDC Review---while nine other sectors archived their primary documents. No test
could see it, because the release manifest hashed the activity files and not the documents
behind them: the guarantee was ``these are the exact numbers'', never ``and here is what they
were read from''. Both documents are archived now, and the class of defect is closed rather
than the instance: \texttt{data/source\_documents.csv} classifies every activity file as
archived, derived, or not-archived; a not-archived file must carry a gap-register row saying
so; and the release manifest hashes the archived documents beside the data. An audit at rc65 found
\num{12} of \num{217} activity files citing at least one document this project does not
hold. The register had read twenty-seven through rc64, and neither count had been what it
seemed. A reviewer's question prompted a test of every claim in both directions, against
both document trees and inside every archive. Of the twenty sources then listed as not
kept, three were on disk the whole time and seventeen had never been saved: the scripts
that fetched them parsed each response in memory, and pages were read without a snapshot.
Those documents were fetched again, copied in from the fetchers' caches or restored from
the repository's history, and each is compared with the table that was read from it. The
same audit found eight files marked archived whose directory did not contain a document
they cite; they are among the twelve. What remains unheld is a priced energy yearbook, a
railway year book its publisher no longer lists, a superseded edition of a facility
tracker, the district tables of the 2017 census, the satellite fire responses behind the
burning proxy, and web pages that could not be captured with their content.
\texttt{data/catalog/not\_retained\_register.csv} names each with the reason and, where one
exists, a held reprint of the same figures. A fetch script now writes what it receives
before it parses it, and a ledger of held documents that only grows fails the test suite
if one is removed.
They are declared now, which is the difference between a limitation and a silence.

\subsection{The national result by IPCC category}

The inventory is computed in ten software modules, and it is reported under twenty-two IPCC
categories. Those are different partitions, and Table~\ref{tab:categorygas} is the one an
inventory reader needs: it is the level at which this work can be compared against Pakistan's
own filings, against EDGAR, or against any other country's inventory. The module view is kept
in Table~\ref{tab:sectors} because it explains where each number is computed and therefore
where an error would live, but it is secondary.

The two partitions differ in one place, and it is the consequential one. Manufacturing
industries and construction (1.A.2) is computed in two modules: the captive self-generation
that the power module reconstructs from metered gas, and the process heat, boilers and
industrial liquid fuels the industry module reads from the petroleum customer classes.
Together they are \SI{28.13}{\mega\tonne}, which is the number to compare against EDGAR's
\SI{43.98}{\mega\tonne} for the same category---and neither module's own total is.

\subsection{Gas composition and the metric choice}

On AR5 the inventory is \SI{160.94}{\mega\tonne} \COtwo{}, \SI{242.93}{\mega\tonne} of
methane and \SI{41.08}{\mega\tonne} of nitrous oxide, expressed as \COe{}. Methane is
\SI{54.6}{\percent} of the national total, which is the single most consequential fact about
Pakistan's emissions profile: it is a country whose inventory is dominated by a short-lived
gas, and therefore one where the choice of metric and time horizon carries unusual policy
weight.

\textbf{This paragraph is now read from a generated ledger, and it is worth saying why.} It
was typed, because until this release no output reported the inventory by gas---every national
output was organised by module. It went stale in place: through three releases it printed a
decomposition whose three parts summed to \SI{455.14}{\mega\tonne} against a headline of
\SI{448.00}{}, with \COtwo{} wrong by nearly \SI{10}{\mega\tonne}, while every headline number
around it was re-anchored. It was recorded as a known gap in the coverage test's declared-debt
list, and the two tests that bound that list both passed throughout, because neither compares
a listed quantity against anything. \emph{A declared debt reads as tracked and behaves as
unchecked.} The debt mechanism has been retired for numeric claims
(Section~\ref{sec:architecture}), the decomposition comes from
\texttt{outputs/national\_category\_gas.csv}, and a test asserts the three gases sum to the
national total under both configurations and both metrics.

The AR5-to-AR6 difference is \SI{6.76}{\mega\tonne}, or \SI{1.5}{\percent} lower. It
decomposes cleanly: methane falls by \SI{8.00}{\mega\tonne} as its GWP moves from \num{28} to
\num{27} for biogenic sources and \num{30} to \num{29.8} for fossil ones, and nitrous oxide
rises by \SI{1.24}{\mega\tonne} (\num{265} to \num{273}), partially offsetting. \COtwo{} is
unchanged by definition. Both metrics are carried through every component row rather than one being derived from the other at the end, which is what
allows the AR5/AR6 pair to be reported at every level of aggregation.

The fossil/biogenic distinction is enforced rather than assumed. Every methane-emitting
component declares which it is, and a contract test asserts that the declaration is present
and that the \COe{} conversion used the corresponding GWP. Only
\SI{23.11}{\mega\tonne}---\SI{9.5}{\percent} of the inventory's methane---is fossil, and it
matters most in the one category that emits both: residential fuel combustion, where dung
and firewood methane is biogenic and natural gas and LPG methane is fossil, within the same
IPCC category and the same output rows.

\subsection{Provincial allocation}

Every sector is allocated to Pakistan's provinces using a category-specific proxy---cropland
for off-road machinery, population for wastewater, provincial fuel sales for
transport, plant-wise generation for electricity, plant capacity for cement---and every
allocation is checked to
reconcile exactly against the national figure it distributes. The contract requires that a
proxy resolve for every emitted category and that an unknown category raise an error rather
than fall back to a default, because a silent default proxy is how a category ends up
allocated by population when population has nothing to do with it.

Two allocations have unusually strong controls. Fugitives use separate provincial production,
pipeline, consumption, refinery and terminal controls rather than one generic proxy; captive
generation rides a 1,105-plant register whose metered gas sums by province,
reconciling with the national OGRA total to within \SI{0.01}{} MMCFD.

A third is worth reporting because it shows the value of writing a proxy's weakness down.
Electricity generation was allocated on a capacity database that this inventory's own
parameter file described as provisional and the weakest element of the sector, and which
named its likely failure: assigning provinces by a coordinate rule ``likely overstates Khyber
Pakhtunkhwa''. Replacing it with plant-wise generation confirmed exactly that. On generation,
Khyber Pakhtunkhwa carries \SI{0.59}{\percent} of the sector's emissions rather than the
\SI{14.24}{\percent} of installed capacity the retired proxy gave it, because its electricity
is overwhelmingly hydro, which burns nothing and belongs nowhere in a combustion sector. The
correction moved roughly \SI{8.8}{\mega\tonne} out of that province at an unchanged national
total. A documented weakness is a specification for the
work that fixes it, which is the same pattern the accounting closures follow in
Section~\ref{sec:closures}.

\subsection{The nine-year series, in two views}
\label{sec:series}

The reference year is the most complete year, not the only one. The engine
reconstructs each sector back to FY2015--16 where its activity record supports it and
publishes the result as \texttt{outputs/pakemis\_national\_series.csv}, with the
sector-by-category-by-gas expansion in
\texttt{outputs/pakemis\_annual\_fy2015\_16\_fy2023\_24.csv}. The product is a
nine-fiscal-year panel in which all ten sector modules report in every year---a complete
90-sector-year rectangle since the FY2015--16 product splits were recovered from the
public record. What is complete is the set of MODULES, not the set of sources: which
categories inside them are estimated is a separate question with its own register, and
Section~\ref{sec:completeness} answers it. Two things about that product matter more
than its magnitudes.

First, coverage is constant at last: every year carries all ten sectors, the national
total runs from \SI{393.84}{\mega\tonne} at FY2015--16 to a maximum of
\SI{483.45}{\mega\tonne} at FY2021--22 (cement was larger that year than in
FY2023--24), and \texttt{coverage\_share\_pct\_of\_reference} reads
\SI{100}{\percent} in every row. Until this release the first year carried eight of
ten sectors and the product warned that differencing across it measured the record
filling in as much as emissions changing; that caveat is retired because its premise
is, not because it was wrong.

How the last two sector-years closed is worth recording in full, because the closure is
an argument about method. For four releases FY2015--16 road transport and industrial
combustion were NE with their observed totals held in
\texttt{outputs/pakemis\_unallocated\_activity\_controls.csv}---Road
\num{12440195} tonnes and Total Industry \num{2023377} tonnes, from the FY2016--17
edition's prior-year column---because the split by product existed in no held document and a sixteen-source
search was logged negative. The search's negative was true of what it crawled and false
of the record: the \emph{Pakistan Oil Report 2015--2016} is publicly served on
ocac.org.pk as a 242-page image flipbook, and its page-50 sectoral table carries the
split. The page images are 602 pixels wide---below reliable OCR---so the table was
transcribed and is accepted only under a four-way identity gate
(\texttt{sectors/transport/ocac\_image\_edition.py}): every row sums to its printed
total, every block to its class total, the six classes to the printed national total,
and---the two-document gate---the recovered Road and Total Industry totals equal the
independently held prior-year column exactly. The controls that recorded the absence now
anchor the recovery. Cells below the glyph floor are committed only where the closing
arithmetic fixes them uniquely, and each carries its read basis
(\texttt{glyph\_identity} or \texttt{identity}) in the committed transcription.

The structural configuration that would have MODELLED the year
(\texttt{FY2015\_16\_FULL\_SCOPE\_BRIDGE}) stays declared and permanently
unpopulated: the observed split made every permitted alternative moot, which is the
strongest argument its contract will ever receive. The validator ships; the bridge never
will.

The other absences closed the same way, and none by acquiring anything unheld. Cement was
reported for the reference year alone until its clinker-trade panel was fetched back to
July 2014 and Table~13.2 of the \emph{Pakistan Statistical Year Book 2020}---held
throughout---supplied the four earliest years. Industrial combustion FY2020--21 was an
image scan whose table is legible once rendered. Declared gaps that turn out to be unread
or unfound documents are the common kind, which is the fifteenth review's point.

Second, the product's \emph{two views} now coincide. The \textsc{harmonized common
scope} view is the trend-safe one: the sectors reconstructed on a consistent method in
every year of the window. Since the FY2015--16 recovery the window opens at the panel's
first year, all ten sectors qualify, and the view runs \SI{393.84}{\mega\tonne} to
\SI{444.96}{\mega\tonne} across the nine years---identical to the full-scope view,
which is what a complete rectangle means. Membership is still derived from the rows
rather than declared: for one release the list was a hand-written tuple that excluded
cement and industry for reasons the data had already overtaken, and a test that checks
only that members are present cannot notice a sector that has become eligible. Both views
continue to ship, because the next partial year would split them again and the machinery
that tells them apart is the guard. Figure~\ref{fig:series} draws them coinciding.

\subsubsection{Waste: a stateful series, not a scaled one}
\label{sec:waste-series}

One property of the panel is easy to miss and easy to under-claim, and an external
review graded it as absent, so it is stated here as a product fact: the waste rows are
\emph{per-year first-order decay}, not a reference-year figure scaled along
population. The deposit history behind the FOD model is rebuilt to each requested year
and the decay recursion run to that year, so every year's solid-waste figure carries
the stock the years before it laid down. Solid-waste disposal runs
\SI{7.593}{} to \SI{9.169}{\mega\tonne} of CH$_4$ across the nine years
(\SI{20.8}{\percent} growth against population growth of roughly two per cent a
year)---the convexity of a decaying stock under a growing deposit stream, which a
scaled series cannot produce---and the waste module total runs \SI{29.33}{} to
\SI{37.45}{\mega\tonne}. The honest boundary of the claim is equally a product fact:
across those years the district population panel varies and, since this release, so do
the sanitation pathway shares, each year reading its own row of the JMP series. Generation and
collection rates and the waste composition are held at their
reference values---the panel's \texttt{held\_constant} register says so on every
row---so the series resolves demographic growth through a stateful model and the recorded
shift between sanitation pathways, and does not
resolve other behavioural or infrastructural change. Year-varying generation and
collection rates await sources
(provincial service statistics) recorded as acquisition
targets, not modelling.

\subsubsection{Agriculture: what a fiscal reconstruction changed, and what it revealed}
\label{sec:agri-basis}

\textbf{Two agricultural histories exist and they answer different questions}, so which is
authoritative is stated rather than inferred. The \emph{fiscal panel} runs FY2015--16 to
FY2023--24 on Pakistani fiscal crop and nitrogen activity, and is the series the national
trend, the nine-year product and the four-product release are built from. The \emph{calendar
diagnostic} runs 1990--2023 on FAOSTAT with a reference-year census anchor, and exists for long
context only: it changes basis at the anchor, so it must not be differenced across that seam.
The join between them is published rather than described, in
\texttt{outputs/pakemis\_series\_splice\_agriculture.csv}, which prices what the change of
basis cost per year and per category. Where this paper reports agriculture, it reports the
fiscal panel.

Agriculture is half the inventory, so its history decides whether the common-scope view can
be differenced at all. Earlier releases drove that history from FAOSTAT and indexed it by
fiscal-end year. Rebuilding it on the Economic Survey's own fiscal tables did not simply
substitute one source for another: it exposed a misalignment of a full calendar year in
\num{3} of the four crops the former history carried.

FAOSTAT labels a crop by its own harvest calendar year. A Pakistani fiscal year running July
to June contains the \emph{kharif} crop harvested in the preceding calendar year and the
\emph{rabi} crop harvested in its own. Reading the whole history at the fiscal-end year was
therefore correct for wheat and a year late for rice, maize and sugarcane; cotton was absent
from the extract altogether. The evidence is not an appeal to the cropping calendar but a
comparison against fiscal tables, in
\texttt{outputs/pakemis\_agriculture\_crop\_calendar.csv}: those three agree with the fiscal
record to within \SI{0.13}{\percent} one year back and disagree by \SIrange{7}{11}{\percent}
where they were read, while wheat behaves in the opposite direction. The concrete cost was
that FY2022--23, the year of the floods, was published with the \emph{following} year's
recovered rice area.

The rebuild is declared per driver rather than per sector, because the two are not the same.
Four categories---rice cultivation, managed soils, urea application and field burning---now
run on observed fiscal crop and nitrogen activity. Enteric fermentation and manure
management, \SI{77.37}{\percent} of the sector's reference-year mass, keep a calendar driver,
because Pakistan publishes no fiscal-year \emph{enumeration} of the national herd: the
Economic Survey's fiscal head counts are, by their own footnote, the inter-census projection
FAOSTAT already carries, and adopting them would buy a label and no observation. The residual
six-month phase error those two categories inherit is measured rather than argued, at under
\SI{2}{\percent} of each category in every year, and it is small for a structural reason---the
series it acts on is a smooth geometric projection, so a phase shift moves the level and
barely touches the trend. The volatility that the seam genuinely distorted lived in the crop
drivers, which are precisely the ones the rebuild fixes.

Across the eight backcast years the change was worth \SI{-7.6969}{\mega\tonne} of basis and
\SI{1.055}{\mega\tonne} of cotton that the former history did not carry, decomposed by year
and category in \texttt{outputs/pakemis\_agriculture\_basis\_change.csv}. The two terms are
never summed into one column: a source correction and a missing category are different claims
with different standing. The reference year does not move, and neither does the national
total that anchors it.

\subsection{Monthly resolution}
\label{sec:monthly}

The same twelve months are also reported one at a time. For every source stream $s$ the layer
asserts
\begin{equation}
E(s,m) = E(s)\cdot p(s,m), \qquad \sum_{m} p(s,m) = 1,
\label{eq:monthly}
\end{equation}
so the twelve months reproduce the annual component exactly and no profile can create or
destroy a gramme. The direction matters: the annual figure remains the control and the
monthly layer only decides \emph{when} within the year, which is what makes a bad seasonal
profile a presentational error rather than an inventory error. All ten modules now carry a
layer, the national concatenation closes against the same component rows every other output
in this paper is built from, and a module that shipped without one would fail rather than
contribute silently.

Resolution is not observation, and the layer is built so that the two cannot be confused.
Every month row carries a temporal class on the same four-level scale as the evidence and
spatial axes: TE1 observed monthly activity for this source and year, TE2 reconstructed from
monthly administrative records, TE3 a process-based seasonal profile, TE4 flat or weakly
informed. Weighted by emissions, the national inventory is \SI{0.9}{\percent} TE1,
\SI{25.29}{\percent} TE2, \SI{19.12}{\percent} TE3 and \SI{54.65}{\percent} TE4
(Figure~\ref{fig:seasonality}). That last figure is the honest headline of this section. A
flat profile is a claim---that a source is aseasonal---and for most of this inventory it is a
claim made because no monthly activity statistic exists, not because the claim was tested.

Where the months are real, two publishers carry most of them. NEPRA's State-of-Industry
Detail-of-Generation listings give the grid its plant-month generation---coal TE2, stated
plant by plant, and gas TE3, where the annual control itself forces the mixed-labelled
combined-cycle group onto gas---so the largest single block of newly timed mass
(\SI{38.09}{\mega\tonne}) follows the fleet's own dispatch. OCAC issues petroleum sales by
customer class monthly, and four modules---road transport, railways, domestic aviation
and industrial combustion---take their shape from it, differencing the cumulative year-to-date
columns that the FY2023--24 editions print. That differencing is an arithmetic identity on
two published numbers rather than a model, which is why those months close to the annual
control exactly; only July, whose cumulative column \emph{is} the month, is read directly,
and it is the inventory's only TE1. Agriculture's synthetic-fertiliser nitrogen rides
NFDC's consolidated offtake by month, published on this inventory's own July--June
fiscal year, so all twelve months are observed in every year and nothing is borrowed
(an earlier edition rode the April--March Annex-7 and borrowed three months; that
path survives only as the declared fallback for a year the consolidated table does
not cover). Rice methane follows cultivation area-days across the provincial
calendars; residential fuel follows province-weighted degree days; cement
rides its own fiscal months in every year---all nine PBS QIM editions are parsed, so no
cement shape is borrowed from anywhere.

That identity is now measured rather than argued. OCAC also publishes a single-month edition
of the same months it later folds into the cumulative columns, and the two are independent
statements of one quantity, so the recovery can be tested against a document that never
entered it. Across four customer classes and three fiscal years,
\num{144} months were compared: \num{132} agree to the tonne, none disagrees by more than
\SI{0.14}{\percent}, and the two largest deviations sit either side of a single month OCAC
restated after the panel was assembled
(\texttt{outputs/ocac\_direct\_edition\_crosscheck.csv}). The check can fail---a one-month
shift injected into the published side, the off-by-one this project has twice found at a
fiscal boundary, is caught by \num{141} of the \num{144} comparisons, and that positive
control is published with the result rather than asserted about it
(\texttt{ocac\_parse\_summary.csv}). Two further products are shipped beside them:
\texttt{ocac\_edition\_currency.csv}, which records for every month whether the shipped panel
is built on the edition OCAC currently serves and flags the one that is superseded, and
\texttt{ocac\_direct\_edition\_quarantine.csv}, which holds the two rows out of \num{6055}
where the source's own products do not sum to the total printed beside them, unrepaired.

Cement is the one stream whose months were rebuilt this release, and the rebuild is worth a
sentence because the shape it replaced was wrong in a measurable direction. All nine fiscal
editions of PBS Table 2 are now parsed rather than the single hand-transcribed calendar-2022
window held before: \num{1017} item-years close on the annual total each row prints for
itself, a rate of \SI{99.12}{\percent}, and the \num{9} that do not are kept unrepaired in
\texttt{outputs/pbs\_qim\_quarantine.csv} because PBS's own arithmetic does not close on them
--- one prints twelve identical monthly values against a stated annual total that is not
twelve times any of them. The check has a
positive control published beside it: \num{932} of the \num{1017} series are distinguishable
from a rotation of their own months, which is the property a seasonal profile actually depends
on, and the remainder are flat series where no parse could tell
(\texttt{pbs\_qim\_parse\_summary.csv}). Two independent things agree with the result --- every month of the earlier hand
transcription reproduces exactly, and the borrowed shape it replaces was overstating cement's
seasonality by roughly half, peaking at \num{2.27} times its trough where FY2023--24 peaks at
\num{1.48} times its own.

Two decisions in the layer are worth stating because the alternative would have closed just
as exactly. The livestock categories---\SI{38.9}{\percent} of the national inventory---ride a
census, which counts a stock once and carries no month at all; they are flat, declared TE4, and
a test asserts that no row whose activity is an enumeration ever receives a shaped profile.
The same rule reaches inside agriculture's largest category: more than half of the nitrogen in
managed soils is deposited or applied off that same census rather than bought from a dealer,
so it is flat too, and putting all of managed soils on the fertiliser shape would have claimed
an observed monthly basis for \SI{15.65}{\mega\tonne} of emissions that has none.

The national year peaks in August at \SI{41.50}{\mega\tonne} and troughs in April at
\SI{32.13}{\mega\tonne}, a ratio of \num{1.29} against a flat \SI{37.08}{\mega\tonne}. That
curvature now has two authors: agriculture---rice's flooded window and the fertiliser
calendar---and, since the grid's plant-month generation arrived, the thermal fleet's summer
dispatch, which deepens the August peak and the spring trough in the same direction. A
reader should take the shape of Figure~\ref{fig:seasonality} as a statement about a handful
of profiles and an assumption about the rest.

\textbf{The whole of that is now published month by month.}
\texttt{outputs/pakemis\_monthly\_coverage\_summary.csv} carries exactly \num{108} rows, one
per month, giving the mass under each temporal class and naming any sector the month does not
have at all; \texttt{pakemis\_monthly\_coverage.csv} backs it at the
month\,$\times$\,sector\,$\times$\,category grain. Read strictly---TE1 and TE2 only, a monthly
\emph{record} of the activity rather than a modelled shape---\SI{18.79}{\percent} of the
nine-year mass is observed and \SI{60.84}{\percent} sits on a declared-flat profile. The single
figure is the least useful thing in the table, because the strong classes are not spread across
the window: the six years before OCAC's panel opens average \SI{13.79}{\percent} observed and the
three after it \SI{28.42}{\percent}---a gap the SIR record first widened (the listings
cover the panel years) and the same archive's earlier sheets then narrowed, labelling
the pre-panel fleet in its own years. No sector-year is missing its months any more: the two
that were---industry and transport in FY2015--16---gained their twelve rows when the year
was recovered, and the \texttt{missing} class waits empty for the next partial year.

The second axis is reported beside it for the first time. Every priced row in the atomic annual
table now declares how well its \emph{quantity} is supported, where \SI{41.2}{\percent} of the
nine-year mass previously carried no class at all---unpoliced by any test and absent from every
output. Derived from the basis each category already declares rather than graded afresh, the
split is \SI{11.1}{\percent} E1, \SI{33.0}{\percent} E2, \SI{54.8}{\percent} E3 and
\SI{1.1}{\percent} E4. That E3 majority is the honest consequence of agriculture: half the
inventory, and eight of its nine years rest on a livestock projection rather than on a count
taken in the period reported.

\emph{Unshaped} no longer means equal twelfths. A stream that declares no seasonality is
declaring something about the SOURCE, and that is not a licence to divide the calendar
wrongly: a continuous emission is proportional to elapsed time, and February is
\SI{10.7}{\percent} shorter than July. Every declared-flat profile is now weighted by its
month's own length, leap days included, which is what widened the peak-to-trough ratio from
\num{1.26} to \num{1.288} without moving a gramme of the annual total. The change is an
arithmetic correction rather than new evidence, and the class of these rows is unchanged. The
ratio has moved again, each time for the opposite kind of reason: to \num{1.32} when
cement stopped borrowing a calendar-2022 shape and took its own fiscal months, and
onward when NEPRA's plant-month generation record put grid coal and gas on
generation-dispatch timing---coal stated plant by plant (TE2), gas assigned by the annual
control's own bound and therefore capped at TE3, never called observed---new evidence about
\SI{38.09}{\mega\tonne} that had sat flat, and not a gramme moved either time.

\subsubsection{Two cross-checks on the timing}

A seasonal profile cannot be validated against the truth: nobody meters when Pakistan's
nitrogen is taken up or when its furnace oil is burned. What can be done is to build the same
profile a second way and publish the disagreement.

The fertiliser profile is the inventory's largest use of an observed monthly series, and the
agronomic alternative is a crop calendar---each crop's share of national nitrogen against its
own application months. The two answer different questions, one recording when urea left the
depot and the other when the crop needs it, and they disagree by \SI{11.5}{} percentage points
in the worst month and \SI{51.5}{} points summed over the year, which is roughly a quarter of
the annual nitrogen changing month (Figure~\ref{fig:monthlychecks}). The offtake series is
markedly flatter than agronomy: it shows no November spike for the wheat basal dressing,
because dealers stock and farmers buy on price, credit and availability weeks before the
field needs it. Neither series is uptake, and the gap between them is the honest width of the
monthly claim for the largest category in the inventory.

The second check tests the differencing method rather than a profile. Summing the twelve
recovered months back to a year is not circular when the annual edition is a separate
publication, and OCAC issues one. For FY2023--24 every customer class agrees to the tonne,
which confirms the ingest and the arithmetic and has no power to detect revision, because
that annual edition reprints the June cumulative column unchanged. FY2021--22 is where the
check bites: its months are twelve separately printed direct editions, reassembling them into
a year is a real test, and they return within \SI{0.34}{\percent} across every class the four
OCAC-driven modules consume.

\subsubsection{What the layer found in the annual rows}

The component schema has carried a declared temporal class since before there was a layer to
use it, which made it a claim about evidence that nothing checked. Reconciling the two moved
\num{34} of \num{202} component rows
(\texttt{outputs/national\_\allowbreak uncertainty\_\allowbreak decomposition.csv}), in
both directions, and the direction is the interesting part. Aviation's TE1 became TE2: FY2023--24's OCAC editions are cumulative, so only
July is read directly and the sector had been claiming an observed monthly basis for the
whole year. Road CNG, bagasse in the power sector, crop-residue burning and the
livestock-origin soil nitrogen all fell to TE4 at that reconciliation, because the shapes
their declarations implied were not then assembled. Two have since climbed back on real
evidence---crop-residue burning on the nine-year fire record, waste disposal on the FOD
state engine---while the sugar crushing season remains documentable and undocumented. Residential charcoal rose to TE3 with the rest of its
sector, where the weak part is the quantity and not the shape. A test now asserts that the
class a component declares is the class its own layer used, so the two cannot drift apart
again.

\subsection{The gridded cube}
\label{sec:cube}

The abstract's gridded claim is discharged by one product built in this repository:
\num{199} of the \num{202} source streams, spread over the national \SI{0.05}{\degree} grid
by the most native geography its evidence supports and over the twelve months by the layer
above; the remaining \num{3} are held at ADM1 because no geometry exists beneath their
provincial control, and closure is therefore raster-plus-unallocated rather than raster
alone. The placed streams follow the identity $E(s,m,g,i) = E(s,g)\,p(s,m)\,w(s,i)$ with $\sum_m p = 1$ and
$\sum_i w = 1$ enforced per stream. The conservation statement is precise about what it
covers: NATIONAL mass is invariant across representations---annual, monthly, gridded,
district and provincial views all close to the same ledger, held by test on every
build---while subnational marginals may differ under documented
physical-versus-administrative allocation rules. The provincial check records the
split exactly: \num{920} stream-province cells exact by construction, \num{15}
unallocated below ADM1---the industry streams whose provincial share is the only spatial
claim they make---\num{45}
carrying a declared divergence (nine streams: domestic aviation, administratively in
ICT and physically at Attock's coordinates, and the six railway non-traction streams,
whose border-yard cells bin across provincial boundaries), and \num{30} derived from
the grid itself and therefore not an independent check. The cube can disagree with
this paper's totals only by failing, never by drifting.

Six artifacts ship. The cube itself
(\texttt{outputs/pakemis\_grid\_0p05\_monthly.nc}) carries the gas$\times$month layers in
native gas mass---no \COe{} per cell, because a GWP is one multiplication and storing it
would be invented precision---together with per-category annual surfaces. It no longer
carries an \texttt{s4\_co2e\_share} layer. The layer once reported the per-cell footprint
of streams spread on a proxy while carrying no native geometry; flagging placed mass is
not the same as declining to place it, so later editions retained such streams at ADM1
instead. rc47 then gave industry its geography outright (kiln points, the clinker
register, built-up), and what remains unallocated is the \num{3} cruise-aviation streams
(\SI{0.06}{\percent} of the national total): fuel burned along corridors no held
evidence can draw, booked at the uplift provinces and published per stream, province, gas
and month in \texttt{pakemis\_spatial\_unallocated.csv}, with the closure identity
carrying the term explicitly: \texttt{ADM1 == ADM2 + unallocated}. That identity is asserted for all nine
fiscal periods and, until this release, its second term was published for one: the cube
computed the unallocated mass every period and wrote only the reference year's, so a reader
checking a historical year's arithmetic had the wrong year's number or none.
\texttt{pakemis\_spatial\_unallocated\_by\_year.csv} carries the same rows with the period
they belong to, and the mass it retains falls from \SI{0.40}{\mega\tonne} in FY2017--18 to
\SI{0.25}{\mega\tonne} in FY2023--24 as facility geography reached more of the inventory.
Nothing above ADM1 moved---the national
total and all \num{1010} provincial marginals are unchanged---because only the placement
\emph{below} the province had been invented. The ADM2$\times$month view
(\texttt{pakemis\_adm2\_monthly.csv})---now also shipped sector-resolved as
\texttt{pakemis\_adm2\_monthly\_sector.csv}, module by shape by gas by month, a
tested partition of the aggregate view; industry appears there carrying only its declared
\texttt{unallocated-below-adm1} absence rows (an empty numeric cell, the reason in its own
column), because the whole module is unplaced and a district
pattern for it would be an invention---the provincial check
(\texttt{pakemis\_grid\_provincial\_check.csv})---which records where the grid marginal is
exact by construction, where it is derived, and where it \emph{declares} divergence, as at
Islamabad International, administratively in ICT and physically in Attock, where the cube
follows the coordinates---and the fallback declarations
(\texttt{pakemis\_grid\_fallbacks.csv}) complete the set. Substitutions are shipped as
data rather than log lines, and so is every deliberate change of placement: the
release's spatial re-bases are measured against the previous commit---shapes moved,
gross and net mass, fallback mass before and after, provincial marginals asserted
unchanged---and published as \texttt{pakemis\_spatial\_rebase\_rc42.csv} and, for this
release's re-bases, \texttt{pakemis\_spatial\_rebase\_rc44.csv}. The second file exists
because the first could not see this release: it reads three reference-year products, so a
re-base that moved FY2017--18 and left FY2023--24 alone reported nothing at all. It now reads
the nine-year district and provincial products as well, and distinguishes the provincial
marginal it must hold fixed from the one a deliberate re-base is allowed to move.

The spatial layer is no longer a single year. The national ledger has carried nine fiscal
years since rc22, while everything below the nation existed for FY2023-24 alone; the same
dispatch is now run for every registered period and published annually as
\texttt{pakemis\_adm1\_by\_year.csv} (\num{7864} rows, stream $\times$ province $\times$
period) and \texttt{pakemis\_adm2\_by\_year.csv} (\num{10350} rows, shape $\times$ gas
$\times$ view $\times$ period). Both close, per year and per gas: the national total equals
the ADM1 marginal, and ADM1 equals ADM2 plus the mass explicitly unallocated below it, to
\num{1e-12} for the eight years the monthly panel controls and to \num{3.3e-8} for the
reference year, that residue being the stream ledger's eight printed decimals against the
panel's twelve and nothing else. What these products do \emph{not} claim is nine years of
observed geography. Each year's national mass is evidenced for that year; the
within-province pattern is the reference year's, rescaled to the year's own control, and it
is graded as such per stream-year rather than in a footnote. The sharpest case is grid
power, whose plant-fuel ledger begins in FY2019--20. The four years before it have no
per-plant fuel evidence at all, so their grid streams ride the built-up substitute, and the
difference is categorical rather than marginal: \SI{17.66}{\mega\tonne} \COe{} is placed on
that substitute in FY2015--16 against \SI{0.62}{\mega\tonne} in the reference year. That
second figure was \SI{0.34}{\mega\tonne} until rc44, and the correction is the more
interesting half of the comparison. A province's whole share used to be pooled across whichever
of its plants happened to carry a coordinate, so fuel belonging to a plant without one was
placed on its located siblings and no substitution was recorded: measured from the ledger,
\SI{3.4}{\percent} of grid gas, \SI{0.0}{\percent} of furnace oil and
\SI{0.0}{\percent} of diesel sat on plants the register cannot place. Furnace oil read
\SI{17.0}{\percent} until rc60, and it did not fall by acquiring a coordinate: the whole
remainder was one station, Lal Pir, which had held a GEM-exact point since rc46 and could not
be matched to it because the plant ledger carried a column heading inside its name. The
substituted mass in the reference year moves from \SI{1.24}{\mega\tonne} to
\SI{0.94}{\mega\tonne} for the same reason. That fraction now
rides the declared surface with an event, which is why thirteen streams move from
\texttt{point\_resolved} to \texttt{point\_partial} in this release: they always were
partial. The cube has always priced its substitutions, but it wrote them for the reference year
alone and only where a province had no located plant at all, so a year that substituted one
province of one stream and a year that substituted an entire sector looked alike in everything
published. \texttt{pakemis\_grid\_fallbacks\_by\_year.csv}
publishes the record for all nine periods, per stream and province, with the mass each
substitution moved.

How much of the nine-year spatial product rests on evidence from the year it describes is
itself published, per stream-year, in
\texttt{pakemis\_spatial\_weights\_by\_year.csv}---the spatial mirror of the temporal
registry's \texttt{borrowed\_from\_year}. Of \num{1818} stream-periods, \num{29} carry no
mass at all (the structural absences above) and the remaining \num{1789} are typed by how
their geography was arrived at: \num{795} (\SI{44.4}{\percent}) were measured for their own
year, \num{615} (\SI{34.4}{\percent}) ride a timeless structural surface, \num{306}
(\SI{17.1}{\percent}) carry the reference year's pattern rescaled, \num{46} rode a substitute
surface, and \num{27} are unallocated below ADM1 in every year. The first two of those were
one category until this release, and separating them corrected the figure downwards: a stream
placed on a \num{2021} land-cover snapshot was counted as measured for its own year in every
one of the nine, and being the reference year does not make a \num{2021} raster an observation
of FY2023--24.

The split between the second and third of those was itself wrong until this release, and the
correction is worth stating precisely because of what it does \emph{not} move. A surface the
engine keys to no period at all has no time axis in any year, so it is timeless in all nine;
the build applied that reading only to the reference year and typed the same surface a
backcast in the other eight. \num{499} stream-years were affected, and their weight vectors
hash identically to the reference year's, so nothing was backcast---there was no
time-specific pattern to backcast from. The label asserted an operation that did not happen,
and a reader counting how much of the map rests on a borrowed \emph{shape} counted all
\num{499} in every year but one. What the repair moves is the \emph{kind} of non-evidence,
not its quantity: the \SI{44.4}{\percent} measured for their own year is unchanged, and the
timeless and backcast classes together still account for \num{921} stream-periods. A
timeless surface and a borrowed one are both silent about the year they are placed in, and
the distinction matters for what would fix them---a backcast needs that year's evidence
read, a timeless surface needs evidence that has a year axis at all.
The registry keeps two axes apart on purpose. Whether a year's geography was \emph{borrowed}
is silent and must be derived; whether it was \emph{substituted} is loud and is read from the
events the build emitted. This pass is the argument for the distinction: the grid fleet was for
a time borrowed from the reference year with nothing recording it, and a register reading only
the emitted record would have called those years well placed. Field burning is the one sector
whose within-province geography is measured in every year, because FIRMS holds all nine fiscal
windows and its proxy is built for the period asked for.

The months below the nation follow the years:
\texttt{pakemis\_adm2\_monthly\_by\_year.csv} carries all \num{108} of them at district
grain (\num{122616} rows), and the reference year's rows in it are byte-identical to the
monthly view that already shipped. The nine gridded cubes are published too, which an
earlier release declined to do. The objection then was that at five hundredths of a degree the
within-province pattern is, for two thirds of stream-years, the reference year's---precision
the evidence does not have, in the format most likely to be read as though it did. That
objection was right and it is answered rather than overridden: every layer now ships beside the
per-stream, per-period evidence in
\texttt{pakemis\_spatial\_weights\_by\_year.csv}, which says whether a period's spatial
pattern is its own or the reference year's carried back, so a reader can see which years a map
actually resolves. A mostly-borrowed raster with an honest provenance column is useful; the same
raster without one is what was refused.

The file is \texttt{pakemis\_grid\_0p05\_annual\_ref\_fy2015\_16\_fy2023\_24.nc}: nine
fiscal periods by three spatial views by three gases on the frozen
\texttt{pk\_0p05\_epsg4326\_v1} lattice, \SI{9.5}{\mega\byte}. Its time coordinate is the
midpoint of each July-to-June interval with explicit bounds, because these are nine fiscal years
and not ten calendar ones---a calendar-year product could be made directly only for
CY2016--CY2023. It carries \texttt{cell\_area} and \texttt{pakistan\_fraction} as variables,
so a cell total in \si{\giga\gram} can be turned into a flux per square kilometre of land
without reconstructing either. Per period and gas the cells plus the retained mass of
\texttt{pakemis\_spatial\_unallocated\_by\_year.csv} reproduce the national total to
within the precision of the stored single-precision raster. Whether a period's map is its own is now measured rather than graded. Each stream-year's
normalised weight vector is hashed where the allocator uses it, and the digest ships in
\texttt{pakemis\_grid\_stream\_surfaces\_by\_year.csv} beside the surface the mass was placed
on. The registry's provenance column was previously derived from what the engine \emph{can}
read for a surface and district panel---the right rule, and still a statement about the build
rather than a measurement of it, which is how a fleet once satisfied one half of that rule
while failing the other for an entire pass unnoticed. The two agree: all \num{306} stream-years
labelled a backcast do carry the reference year's pattern, and of the \num{795} labelled as
resting on their own year's evidence, none does. The hash also reaches past the label, which is
how the mislabelling above was found rather than argued: \num{805} non-reference stream-years
carry the reference year's pattern, \num{306} of them typed a backcast and \num{499} typed
timeless, and it was the second group---identical patterns under two different names---that
showed the label was describing the period rather than the surface. The check runs in both directions, because a
grading that can only be wrong one way is a grading nobody checks. Monthly
cells and the ledger configuration remain the reference year's alone: neither a historical month's
seasonal geography nor a historical ledger configuration is gridded, and neither is invented.

One frame carries all nine periods, and the products say so in their own headers: the
canonical \num{152}-unit set on \texttt{geoBoundaries-gbOpen-PAK-ADM2-2019}. Eleven of those
units were carved out of larger districts during the window, so an earlier year's row for such
a district is the mass that would fall inside \emph{today's} boundary rather than a figure any
administration reported at the time. That is the only choice under which the nine years are
comparable with one another, but it is a choice; the contemporaneous administrative geography
is not held, and the frame's own date column carries source labels rather than dates, so the
gap is registered as an acquisition request instead of estimated. 

The nine years reach the query surface, which is where they were always needed: this pass began
because a nine-year ledger was attached to a one-year renderer, and products that never enter
the semantic release leave that exactly as it was.
\texttt{pakemis\_adm2\_sector\_by\_year.csv} carries the district view with its module axis
for every period, and the release publishes it as \texttt{fact\_adm2\_year} beside the
reference year's \texttt{fact\_adm2\_month}. Two tables rather than one, deliberately: making
the month table cover nine years at module grain would store a borrowed within-province pattern
a hundred and eight times per district instead of nine, and a single table whose module
coverage was complete for one year and absent for eight would answer a comparison across years
wrongly while answering the reference year correctly. The split puts that asymmetry in the
schema, where a consumer meets it. A district query for an earlier year is now served rather
than refused---the refusal it used to raise, ``spatial products exist for FY2023-24 only'', had
become false---while a \emph{monthly} district request outside the reference year is still
refused, now for the reason that is actually true. The provincial level follows the same
arc one release later: \texttt{fact\_adm1\_year} publishes the nine-year ADM1 product
(\texttt{pakemis\_adm1\_by\_year.csv}) at stream grain with exact CO\textsubscript{2}e
---ADM1 is the level the closure identity holds exactly at, so it carries no bounded
cells---retiring the last stale spatial refusal, which had provinces answering monthly
for the reference year and not at all for the other eight. This is a reconstruction
of where past emissions most likely sat, and its value is that a reader can see, per row,
which part of it was measured.

The allocation now also prices its own alternatives where an admissible second reading
exists. \texttt{data/stream\_spatial\_alternatives.csv} registers, per allocation
basis, whether another defensible proxy exists, and
