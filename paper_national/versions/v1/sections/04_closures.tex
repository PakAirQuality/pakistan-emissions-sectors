\section{Four Enforced Accounting Closures}
\label{sec:closures}
% ===========================================================================

Four quantities in this inventory are estimated once and consumed twice across an accounting
boundary. In each case the conventional treatment is for each side to estimate its own share
and for a methods document to assert that the two agree. Here each is a single number computed
once and consumed twice, and the equality is asserted with \texttt{==} rather than with a
tolerance, because a tolerance would permit exactly the drift the closure exists to prevent.

The boundary is not the same kind of boundary in all four cases, and the distinction matters
for what the technique transfers to. Two are \emph{transfers between modules}: a quantity
leaves one sector's books and must arrive on another's. Two are \emph{partitions within one
module}: a single total is divided between categories that are reported separately, and the
parts must exhaust the whole. Calling all four cross-sector, as an earlier edition of this
paper did, described the architecture more grandly than it works.

The four are the closures enforced with \texttt{==}; they are not the whole of the
inventory's cross-boundary accounting. The release publishes \num{8} ledgers in one schema
(\texttt{data/cross\_sector\_ledger\_map.csv}, projected into the validation package's
cross-sector registry with every value read from the sector ledger it points to), each with
its control, its transfers and its unresolved rows: the four here, and the delivered-gas
ledger across the metered modules---whose net-of-leakage alternative is priced in
\texttt{outputs/national\_\allowbreak gas\_\allowbreak closure\_\allowbreak variant.csv},
one row per configuration with the burned-and-leaked volume and the leaked share behind
it---the fertiliser feedstock-to-urea ledger, the livestock
nitrogen-excretion ledger and the crop-residue ledger, whose closures are asserted in their
own modules' tests. An earlier edition counted only the four.

All four were known defects before they were closures. Each is documented as a limitation in
an earlier version of this inventory---an orphaned flow, a double-counted fuel, an
unpartitioned total---which is the point: the closures are not new physics but the conversion
of four written-down warnings into four enforced properties.

\subsection{Dung-cake nitrogen: agriculture to residential}
\label{sec:closure-dung}

Pakistani households burn cattle and buffalo dung as fuel. The agriculture sector has always
accounted for this on the supply side, removing \SI{28}{\percent} of cattle and
\SI{30}{\percent} of buffalo excreted nitrogen from the manure-management balance and
reporting it with the notation key IE---included elsewhere---against the Energy sector. Until
the residential sector existed, nothing was elsewhere. The nitrogen left agriculture's books
and arrived nowhere: \SI{1.260}{\mega\tonne} of excreted nitrogen a year, correctly removed
from one balance and never entered in another.

The residential sector now receives it, and receives it as mass rather than as an estimate.
The transfer runs through the same herd, the same species categories and the same
burned-fuel shares that drive the agricultural removal, converted from nitrogen to volatile
solids using IPCC's VS rates for the same animal categories and region that supply the
nitrogen rates. The result is \SI{38.77}{\mega\tonne} of \emph{volatile solids}, which corresponds to
\SI{58.74}{\mega\tonne} of air-dry dung cake under the declared moisture and ash basis.
Volatile solids rather than total dung mass is the quantity the closure runs on, because VS is
the organic fraction and dung ash carries no energy---but the two are not interchangeable, and
an earlier edition called the VS figure ``dry matter burned as household fuel'', which reads
as the mass a household actually handles and is \num{1.5} times smaller than it.

The transfer fixes the diverted mass and not its destination. Of what the residential
ledger receives, the reference burns in
households the dung the household survey observes, \SI{10.93}{\mega\tonne} of air-dry cake,
and the other \SI{47.81}{\mega\tonne} of the diversion has no observed destination: it is a
typed unresolved row outside the total, with no emission applied, and
Section~\ref{sec:bridge} prices its candidate destinations. Every release through rc66
burned the whole diversion in households; that reading is retained beside
\texttt{REF\_2023\_24} as configuration~L, a
destination assumption for the diverted dung rather than a reading of household use.

The identity is exact by construction: the diverted mass is a derivation from
agriculture's own flow, and household combustion plus the unresolved remainder equals it.
A change to the manure-management shares moves the diversion and the remainder together.

The biogenic \COtwo{} from this fuel is reported as a memo item outside the national total,
following IPCC's biomass convention and Pakistan's own stated practice in BUR-1
\citep{pakbur1}. Its \CHfour{} and \NtwoO{} are counted: incomplete combustion is a real net
source regardless of the carbon's origin.

\subsection{Household generator petrol: carved out of the road control total}
\label{sec:closure-generator}

Pakistan's petroleum statistics report motor gasoline sold to a Road customer class. Not all
of it moves a vehicle. Households running standby generators against load-shedding buy their
petrol from the same forecourts and it is booked to the same class, so a road inventory
anchored on that control silently includes stationary domestic generation, and a residential
inventory that also estimates generator fuel counts it twice.

The residential sector derives the quantity---\SI{56.33}{\kilo\tonne} of petrol in
FY2023--24---from HIES generator-fuel expenditure divided by the administered pump price,
and the transport sector subtracts exactly that figure from its road petrol control by
calling the residential module rather than by holding a copy of the number. The two cannot
drift apart, and the direction is the one that matters: the fuel is not deleted, it is
reassigned from 1A3b to 1A4b, so the national total is unchanged and only the attribution
moves.

This closure replaced an earlier one. Until the transport sector was re-anchored on the
petroleum statistics' own customer classes, it took a single national diesel supply figure as
its control and subtracted the railway fuel account from it. That subtraction is now
unnecessary---Railways is its own customer-class row, outside the Road row by
construction---so the railway account was freed to do something better, and appears in
Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks} as an independent cross-source check rather than as an internal
correction. A closure that becomes structurally impossible is a closure that has been
superseded by better source data, which is the outcome to want.

\subsection{One waste stream, partitioned twice}
\label{sec:closure-waste}

Municipal solid waste generation is a single national quantity---\SI{31.56}{\mega\tonne} a
year---that must be divided between the waste that reaches a disposal site and the waste that
does not. Collection is \SI{50.3}{\percent}, so \SI{15.88}{\mega\tonne} enters the solid-waste
disposal model as the current year's addition to the first-order-decay stock, and
\SI{15.67}{\mega\tonne} remains uncollected, of which \SI{60}{\percent} is openly burned.

\textbf{The partition is two-stage, and the second stage does not close into two parts.}
Generation splits into collected and uncollected, $G = D + U$; the uncollected splits again
into what is burned and what is not, $U = B + O$, and $O$---\SI{6.27}{\mega\tonne} dumped to
land and waterways---is a declared unresolved fate rather than a category. Reporting disposal
and burning as though they exhausted the stream would imply an inventory of \emph{everything}
generated, which this is not.

The two categories are computed from one generation figure and the partition is asserted to
be exhaustive: disposed plus uncollected equals generated, exactly. The failure this prevents
is the natural one. Open burning and solid-waste disposal are usually estimated from separate
literature---a per-capita burning rate here, a landfill tonnage there---and nothing then
forces their sum to be a quantity of waste the country actually generates. Under this
construction, an argument about the collection rate moves emissions between two categories
without changing how much waste exists.

\subsection{One diesel total, partitioned between on-road and off-road}
\label{sec:closure-offroad}

The same road-class diesel row supports tractors, agricultural plant and---through the
source data's residual bucket---construction machinery, which IPCC reports under 1A4c~ii
and 1A2\,gvii and not under road transport. The transport sector's fleet model normalises
vehicle-kilometre shares over all vehicle classes \emph{including} the off-road class,
deliberately: renormalising after dropping off-road would hand the on-road classes
\SI{100}{\percent} of the diesel while off-road separately claimed its own share,
counting the same tonne twice.

Mixed off-road machinery is therefore a reported category of \SI{1.73}{\mega\tonne}
rather than the memo item it was, its name declaring the 1A4c/1A2 mixture, and the memo is
asserted to be empty so that the same diesel cannot appear in both places. Since the
August 2026 re-anchor the road control is OCAC's Road customer-class row, so the bulk
non-road diesel claimants (industry, utilities, government, railways) are excluded by the
source's own partition, and only the retail-channel agricultural and captive claims remain
inside---carried as unresolved status rows of the transport fuel ledger.

\subsection{The gas ledger, and the double-claim it prices}

The natural-gas closure is the sharpest of the four, because its finding is a
double-claim rather than a partition: the post-meter leakage factor rides delivered
gas that four combustion claims burn \emph{in full}. OGRA's Table~3.31 partitions
every delivered molecule by end use, and
\texttt{outputs/national\_gas\_closure.csv} joins each class to the quantity the
consuming module actually burns---the same publication's number for residential,
general industry, cement, captive power and CNG (the build aborts if any pair
drifts past rounding), and a printed different-meter reconciliation for grid power,
whose NEPRA TOE activity is another publication's meter. On the
\num{18685} million m$^3$ leak base, about \SI{114}{\kilo\tonne} of methane is
claimed as leakage against delivered gas---about \SI{1.0}{\percent} of the same
whole-gas mass. Until this release the reference booked that gas as combusted
\emph{in full}, keeping both claims and saying so; the physical-atmosphere
identity is now enforced in the booking itself: the four combustion claims book
their gas rows net of the leaked whole-gas share, all three gases, because gas
that leaves upstream of the burner tip produces none of them. The leaked
molecules stay in the inventory where they physically go, as 1.B.2 fugitive
methane---the netting removes a double-claim, not emissions. The administrative
fuel-accounting view, in which the full metered delivery is booked as
combusted for reconciliation against fuel-sales statistics, survives as the
named joint configuration
\emph{gross\_delivered\_gas\_accounting} (\SI{+0.288}{\mega\tonne}), applied to
the gas rows the four modules book net. Two defects in the share's earlier
derivation nearly cancelled and are corrected in the variant file: the leaked
share divided methane mass by whole-gas mass, and the \COtwo{} base was
imputed from delivered volumes rather than read from the modules. The
sharpest inconsistency the ledger surfaced is removed from the reference in this
release: through rc66 fertilizer feedstock leaked at a combustion-derived
factor while being deliberately outside every combustion module. The factor is
defined on gas consumed in combustion equipment, so the ledger now carries the
fertilizer class as two rows. Feedstock---\SI{83.5}{\percent} of the class on
NFDC's metered feed and fuel gas, and \SI{25.9}{\percent} of the leak base as it
stood---is outside the base; process fuel, which is burned, stays inside it, its
carbon booked by 2.B.1 on production. The earlier membership is retained and not
defended as the fugitives scenario \emph{post\_meter\_including\_feedstock},
which adds \SI{1.195}{\mega\tonne}; the \SI{+0.288}{\mega\tonne} above contains
no fertilizer term and is not the size of this correction. The ledger does not
close the fertilizer stream's carbon: a gas-carbon potential of
\SIrange{13.2}{14.7}{\mega\tonne} \COtwo{} at the utility heating-value band
stands against \SI{8.08}{\mega\tonne} gross booked by 2.B.1 on ammonia,
published in \texttt{outputs/pakemis\_gas\_flow\_crosswalk.csv} as an open difference and assigned to no cause. The identity
also holds per draw on the combustion side: the residential, CNG, industrial
and captive delivered volumes share one activity draw through the cross-module
dependency group \texttt{delivered\_gas\_ogra\_3\_31}, while the leak side's
draws are separately injected and that decorrelation is declared rather than
hidden---the grid volume rides its own NEPRA meter, reconciled in the ledger,
never the shared draw.

\subsection{What closure does and does not buy}

None of these four operations makes the inventory more accurate in the sense of moving it
closer to a measured truth; there is no measurement to move towards. What they buy is that a
particular class of error becomes impossible rather than merely unobserved. A tonne of diesel
cannot be burnt twice. A kilogram of excreted nitrogen cannot both leave the manure balance
and fail to arrive in the dung ledger, as household fuel or as its typed unresolved
remainder. A tonne of municipal waste cannot be both landfilled and openly
burned, nor can it be neither.

It is worth being precise about the residual risk, because closure invites overconfidence.
An exhaustive partition guarantees that the parts sum to the whole; it says nothing about
whether the split point is right. The waste collection rate of \SI{50.3}{\percent} carries
real uncertainty, and moving it moves emissions between two categories with quite different
methane yields. The off-road class inherits its source workbook's residual bucket---the
catch-all ``Other vehicles'' class maps into it wholesale---so its magnitude is a genuine
carve-out of the fuel total while its composition is soft. These are stated as limitations in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, and the closures are what make them visible as questions about
a split rather than invisible as disagreements between two independent estimates.
